## A price list for operations that did not exist yet

[Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/) took effect at the start of epoch 526. What it put into the protocol was a price list. Cardano charges a smart contract for every operation it performs, and an operation with no price in the cost model cannot be used at all, so the 12 new operations the coming hard fork would add to Plutus needed entries before any contract could call them. This update wrote those entries and changed nothing about the price of anything already in use.

The order is the document's own. The operations themselves, bitwise arithmetic and a hash function that Bitcoin relies on, were not yet available: they arrive only when the hard fork raises the protocol version to 10, and the document says the prices were filed ahead of it so that the operations could be used as soon as the hard fork went through, with no vote still pending. The committee had ratified it one epoch earlier, the Cardano Atlantic Council noting in its yes vote that the changes matched what had been circulated off chain and had already run safely on the test networks. The [review of epochs 523 to 525](/governance-review/epochs-523-525/) has that vote, including the withdrawn first attempt at the same update which expired at the same boundary.

## The name of the coming hard fork

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) spent its last three epochs of voting here. It was filed by a hard fork working group at Intersect and asks for that hard fork to carry the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang, and places the name in a tradition of naming hard forks in memory of contributors to Cardano. The tally the record stores for it, read after it closed at the start of epoch 529, is 76.54% of DRep power, with six committee yes votes and one abstention as of the close of epoch 528. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. Within this three epoch window, most of the power behind yes ballots was cast in epoch 528: ₳353.0M at that epoch's readings, against ₳57.4M across epochs 526 and 527 together. Over its whole run from epoch 522 the late epoch accounts for about 29% of the yes power cast. That is a statement about when power was cast, not about how support grew, since a late ballot from a large DRep moves the epoch figure without changing anyone else's position.

The reasons on file are short and personal. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/), voting yes in epoch 528, wrote that there was "not much more to add". [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) had voted yes in epoch 524 because the name had "widespread support", and [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) in epoch 525 because the man "embodied the values and ideals of the community". jonahkoch wrote in epoch 527 of his journey from traditional finance to blockchain developer, and Ha-Nguyen in epoch 528 that he had founded the first fully dollar backed stablecoin on Cardano. The one published abstention, from Wada DRep in epoch 527, argued that the old name "has already traveled" with the upgrade so close and that a later hard fork would be a better dedication. The Eastern Cardano Council abstained as a committee member, citing its position that the committee should not rule on information actions that do not measure sentiment for a future on chain action. The [review of epochs 529 to 534](/governance-review/epochs-529-534/) has the result.

## Delegated power moves ₳376.5M in one epoch

At the epoch 528 reading, 592 DReps held ₳2.14B of delegated power, up ₳399.9M from ₳1.74B at epoch 526 and the highest reading up to then. ₳376.5M of that came between the last two readings alone. No ada was created by this: delegated power is stake that someone has pointed at a DRep, and the record shows the net rise between two readings without identifying where it came from. Voting activity did not jump with it. The record lists 205 DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs, up from 192 and the most up to then, while 392 of the 592 holding delegated power had no recorded ballot in that period, and the ten largest DReps held 44.40% of the power at epoch 526 and 44.79% at epoch 528. The window recorded 98 ballots across the roles, superseded votes included, 67 from DReps, 26 from pools and 5 from committee members, with final ballots from 65 distinct DReps and 26 pools. The treasury reading rose ₳8.9M to ₳1.62B, with no withdrawal paid out of it.
