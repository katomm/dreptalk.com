## A price list for operations that did not exist yet

[Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/) took effect at the start of epoch 526. What it put into the protocol was a price list. Cardano charges a smart contract for every operation it performs, and an operation with no price in the cost model cannot be used at all, so the 12 new operations the coming hard fork would add to Plutus needed entries before any contract could call them. This update wrote those entries and changed nothing about the price of anything already in use.

The order is the document's own. The operations themselves, bitwise arithmetic and a hash function that Bitcoin relies on, were not yet available: they arrive only when the hard fork raises the protocol version to 10, and the document says the prices were filed ahead of it so that the operations could be used as soon as the hard fork went through, with no vote still pending. The committee had ratified it one epoch earlier and the [review of epochs 523 to 525](/governance-review/epochs-523-525/) has that vote, including the withdrawn first attempt at the same update which expired at the same boundary.

## The name of the coming hard fork

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) spent its last three epochs of voting here. It was filed by a hard fork working group at Intersect and asks for that hard fork to carry the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang, and places the name in a tradition of naming hard forks in memory of contributors to Cardano. As of the close of epoch 528 it stood at 76.54% of counted DRep power, with six committee yes votes and one abstention as of that close. Most of the DRep power that voted did so at the last opportunity: the yes ballots of epoch 528 amounted to ₳353.0M at that epoch's readings, against ₳57.4M across epochs 526 and 527 together. That is a statement about when power was cast, not about how support grew, since a late ballot from a large DRep moves the epoch figure without changing anyone else's position. The [review of epochs 529 to 534](/governance-review/epochs-529-534/) has the result.

## Delegated power moves ₳376.5M in one epoch

At the epoch 528 reading, 592 DReps held ₳2.14B of delegated power, up ₳399.9M from ₳1.74B at epoch 526 and the highest reading up to then. ₳376.5M of that came between the last two readings alone. No ada was created by this: delegated power is stake that someone has pointed at a DRep, so the jump is stake moving from holders who had not delegated to one, and the record does not say whose. Voting activity did not jump with it. The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 192 to 205, the most up to then but still about a third of the 592, and the ten largest DReps held 44.40% of the power at epoch 526 and 44.79% at epoch 528. The window recorded 98 ballots across the roles, superseded votes included, 67 from DReps, 26 from pools and 5 from committee members, with final ballots from 65 distinct DReps and 26 pools. The treasury reading rose ₳8.9M to ₳1.62B, with no withdrawal paid out of it.
