## The same change, refused a second time

[CARDANO BLOCKCHAIN ECOSYSTEM CONSTITUTION v2.3](/ga/c882f194684d672316212f01efc6d28177e8965b7cd6956981fe37cc6715961e00/) expired at the start of epoch 593 with 64.85% of counted DRep power behind it, on 173 yes ballots, 29 no and 10 abstain, and five committee members in favour with two abstaining. A new constitution needs 75%.

What it wanted is worth stating plainly, because this is the second time the series reports a constitution vote and the second time the substance is at risk of disappearing behind a version number. Its central change was the one the [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/) described: removing the budget information action as a separate step and folding its requirements into the treasury withdrawal action itself. Under the rule as it stood, a project needed a budget endorsed first and a payment approved second, and the committee had already refused two requests for skipping the first step.

Around that, the revision added things the earlier attempt had not. It would have deleted provisions its own text calls aspirational rather than enforceable, among them codes of conduct for DReps, stake pool operators and the committee itself. It would have added definitions for terms the constitution uses without defining, such as active voting stake and net change limit. And it would have required a proposal's documents to stay immutable after submission, and applied the treasury audit safeguards to every withdrawal rather than to some of them: the duties to appoint independent auditors, publish reports, designate an administrator and keep the money in separate auditable accounts had applied only to withdrawals above ₳1M in a two year period, and the revision would have made them the general rule.

The immutability clause is the one that answers a problem the series has already run into. A committee member had rejected an earlier proposal because two of its references were not hashed and could therefore be changed after the vote, which an [earlier edition](/governance-review/epochs-548-553/) covers. This revision would have written that requirement into the constitution.

## A late block of no weight

The no side arrived late. Across the six epochs from its submission the revision had collected at most ₳0.06B of no weight in any single epoch. In epoch 592, its last full epoch, ₳1.11B voted no.

That is a real pattern and it is not the same as a cause. A share of counted power is not reconstructed here for the earlier boundaries, so there is no basis for saying the revision would have carried without those ballots: a proposal that stood above 75% for six epochs and then fell would need that arithmetic shown, and this edition cannot show it. What can be said is that a dozen ballots from large DReps in a final epoch moved the weight more than two hundred ballots before them.

## The tools do not get paid

[Withdraw ₳1,150,000 for GovTool 12 months active maintenance and development](/ga/d2db60c5307cb517c735e2d0138d2b6f10fc5b221d610fa187719bdc82af9a0300/) expired at the start of epoch 591 with 55.96% of counted DRep power behind it, on 142 yes ballots to 37 no, with five committee members for it and one against. A treasury withdrawal needs 67%.

It is the second half of a pair. The budget was endorsed in the [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/), covering a year of maintenance and development on the open source applications most DReps use to register, delegate, submit proposals and vote, run by a consortium of the builders behind them. The withdrawal's own document names that budget by id, says Intersect would administer the money through a smart contract and return anything unused, and itemises the sum: hosting and infrastructure, development of each part of the tools priced in full time equivalents, ₳150,000 for contributors paid by their contributions and ₳100,000 for other open source governance tools built on the same code. The budget cleared its vote because a budget has no bar to clear. The payment did not, because a payment does.

The two bars are the point. A constitution needs 75% of counted DRep power, a treasury withdrawal 67%, and a budget or any other information action needs nothing at all from the ledger: it records a share and stops. Three of the proposals in this window ran into three different versions of the same electorate, and only the one with no bar came out ahead.

## Still open

Four proposals were open at the close, all filed in epochs 589 and 590, and the [review of epochs 588 to 590](/governance-review/epochs-588-590/) describes each from its own document. The committee pay proposal is a ₳1M budget for five of the seven members, up to ₳200,000 each for the 73 epoch term. The listing loan request is the ₳5M withdrawal that follows the loan budget, at 2.44% interest with the balance due within five years. The deposit refund asks for ₳103,000 to make good a ₳100,000 deposit lost to a node bug. And the domain plan asks for no money, only for endorsement before the Cardano Foundation applies for the .ada and .cardano domain endings at its own expense. Their deadlines fall at the start of epochs 596 and 597, and the [review of epochs 594 to 596](/governance-review/epochs-594-596/) has the first of them.

The window recorded 443 ballots, 428 from DReps, 6 from pools and 9 from committee members, with final ballots from 152 distinct DReps. The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.63B at epoch 593 across 858 DReps, the ten largest holding 49.34% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳8.1M to ₳1.63B, with nothing paid out of it since epoch 578 and ₳78.0M of the ₳350M ceiling still unused.
