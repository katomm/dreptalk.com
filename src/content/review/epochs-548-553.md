## Two ways to cap what the treasury may spend

Nothing could be paid out of the treasury until the DReps agreed a ceiling, and two proposals offered different ones. [Set 2025 Net Change Limit of 300M ADA, 2026 Net Change Limit of 250M ADA](/ga/7f320409d9998712ff3a3cdf0c9439e1543f236a3d746766f78f1fdbe1e06bf800/) asks for ₳300M this year and ₳250M the next, sized to what the treasury is forecast to take in, and settles both years in one vote. [2025 Net Change Limit](/ga/9b62b3c632f329016a968ac25211825bb4f84b12461121c7da3aa11df92370f900/) asks for ₳350M from the start of epoch 532 to the end of epoch 604, a range its text calls 72 epochs although it covers 73, sized to the previous year of actual income, and leaves the year after to its own decision. As of the close of this window it was still being voted on.

The two documents also disagree on what counts as agreement. The constitution asks for a limit to be agreed by more than 50% of the active voting stake, and the single year proposal takes that figure as its bar. The two year proposal names 67% instead, the share a treasury withdrawal needs, on the reasoning that the ceiling on withdrawals should clear the same bar as the withdrawals themselves. Its authors describe themselves as community members unconnected to the founding entities, filing because in their view every further epoch without a limit costs the ecosystem. The single year figure was agreed with the Cardano Budget Committee and rests on what the treasury actually took in over epochs 459 to 531, ₳336.0M, within 5% of the ₳350M it proposes. At 34.73% the two year proposal fell short of either figure, and at 73.35% the single year one was past both in its final epoch of voting.

The two year proposal closed at the start of epoch 553 with 34.73% of DRep power behind it, on 138 yes ballots, 112 no and 14 abstain. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. The arithmetic deserves a second look, because ₳1.69B of delegated power voted yes on it and only ₳1.62B voted no. A share of counted power is not yes weight against no weight: abstaining power drops out, and every ada of delegated power that never cast a ballot is counted on the no side, along with the power parked on the always no confidence option. The power that cast no ballot and the always no confidence delegation pulled the share down to 34.73% even though yes votes narrowly outweighed no votes.

## Two years at once, and a file that changed

The published no rationales were mostly about the second year and the proposal's documentation rather than the sum. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) wrote that it agreed more with that figure than with the alternative, but that setting the limit for two years at once was not the right approach and there was "no need to rush" a limit for the second year. [SASA_nagamaru_ながまる](/dreps/drep1ytuddrgqca0xd4aams6pl9zndpcmxg0rs7z3mggjelm6kscp4arg5/) called the basis of the numbers clear and voted no because a ceiling fixed a year ahead would make it "difficult to respond flexibly to unexpected future changes". The Tempo DRep said the same in one line. Martin Lang voted no for lack of a definition: nothing said whether a net change limit is the sum of withdrawals or the change in the balance including inflows, or when its year begins. Dave found both figures "excessively high" for a governance system this young. And Hephaestus Stake Pool, which preferred these figures to ₳350M, voted no because the proposal's metadata file was altered within 48 hours of submission, producing a mismatching hash and "confusion among voters".

The yes side took the figures as the point. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/), the largest DRep, preferred this proposal because income in the current year is clearly lower than the year before and because the next year should be settled at the same time, and voted yes on both limits so that one would exist. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) voted yes while preferring lower figures still, 280,000,000 ada and 230,000,000, on the principle that the limit "does not exceed the expected revenues of the Treasury". [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) called the numbers reasonable and the community origin the kind of governance culture it hopes for.

The committee went four to one against it, with one abstention and one member casting no ballot at all, a share of 16.67%, and the four no votes cited the proposal's metadata and the approval bar it set itself, not the amounts. Input Output found it procedurally unconstitutional because its rationale did not hash the contents of two of its references, so they could be changed afterwards without a trace, while applauding the initiative. The Intersect Constitutional Council pointed at the same file from the other end: the metadata anchor was changed within the first 48 hours, the confusion among voters was enough for a majority of its members to find the principle of voting "protected from undue influence and manipulation" violated, and the wording of the proposal itself was constitutional, as multi year limits are. Emurgo objected to the bar: the proposal sets its own threshold at 67% where the constitution defines more than 50% of the active voting stake. The Cardano Atlantic Council read the same sentence as an attempt "to modify the ratification thresholds dictated by the Cardano Constitution". Hephaestus Stake Pool, voting as a DRep, disagreed with that reading: a proposal holding itself to a higher bar still clears the constitutional one.

## The single year limit as of the close

Its rival was in its final epoch of voting, which ended at the start of epoch 554. As of the close of epoch 553 the single year ₳350M proposal stood at 73.35% of DRep power, and the ballots already cast on it show where the argument had settled. As of that close YUTA had voted yes in epoch 548, having preferred the other proposal, because the worst outcome would be no limit at all, and the Eternl DRep Committee had voted yes in epoch 552 with ₳350M "higher than we would have preferred". SIPO changed its vote to yes in epoch 553, writing that after the two year proposal failed, a second failure "would effectively halt all budget-related actions" for the year. Cerkoryn voted no in epoch 551 because a limit that high "risks incentivizing the community to withdraw funds up to the maximum". The [review of epochs 554 to 556](/governance-review/epochs-554-556/) has the result.

## The roadmap closes with the committee in three minds

[Defining the Cardano Vision and Roadmap for 2025 and beyond](/ga/56f39054758f1a3cedc1de9225d66bf270b62dfdbfbc5399f1d6d43aceffc63600/) closed at the start of epoch 549 with 63.81% of DRep power behind it, on 243 yes ballots, 9 no and 21 abstain. It asked DReps to endorse an order of priorities drawn up by the product committee of Intersect: scaling the main chain, pulling liquidity in from other chains, building out the second layer, better developer tooling, programmable assets and several independent implementations of the node. Pools added 37 yes ballots and 2 abstentions, a yes stake share of 3.57%.

The yes votes read their own priorities into it. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) found the initiatives "in line with Yoroi's future development" as an open source product focused on usability. SASA was "very excited" about parallel block production and about a validation service layer that could connect privacy focused chains. CardanoYoda voted yes with the objection that scalability "is addressed from three sides, which might be unnecessary". AdaStat voted yes despite its doubts about a reference link "that may change at any time", because a temperature check does not change the protocol.

The committee split three ways, three yes, one no and three abstentions, for a share of 75%, which on an opinion vote decides nothing either way, and its members disagreed about whether they should be voting at all. The Intersect Constitutional Council found the action "not constitutional" on a split of four to none with one abstention, for the same reason AdaStat had shrugged off: the primary reference is a website that can change, so the roadmap is not fully captured in immutable metadata, although the content it reviewed "was not found to contain anything unconstitutional". The Eastern Cardano Council abstained on principle, writing that the committee should not vote on information actions that do not measure sentiment for future on chain actions. The Cardano Atlantic Council abstained because "imprecise language" left it unclear whether the action was a poll or a request for feedback.

## A rolling count peaks, and delegation keeps climbing

At epoch 548, 409 DReps had a ballot in the previous twelve epochs, the highest count up to then, and by epoch 553 it read 352. Neither figure is a count of DReps that voted in this window: the measure looks back twelve epochs, so the constitution and treasury tax votes of epochs 537 to 545 were still inside it at the start and dropping out of it by the end. Within the window itself, 300 distinct DReps cast a final ballot.

The total power delegated to DReps reached ₳4.82B at epoch 553, the highest reading up to then and up from ₳4.18B at epoch 548, with ₳298.9M of the rise falling between epochs 549 and 550 alone. Concentration went with it: the ten largest DReps held 41.29% of the total at epoch 548 and 47.32% at epoch 553. The window recorded 684 ballots, superseded votes included, 527 from DReps, 137 from pools and 20 from committee members. The treasury reading was ₳1.73B at epoch 553, ₳22.4M above epoch 548 and the highest up to then, with no withdrawal yet enacted through the governance process.
