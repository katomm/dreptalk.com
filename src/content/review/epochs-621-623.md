## A framework, and two votes against it

[Cardano Budget Process Framework (facilitated by Intersect)](/ga/e9693ed6b6465b2b46daaf641486a12b4b2946081634b5967b9d98897b0598fa00/) asks for no money. What it asks for is agreement on how money will be asked for in future, and it is specific about the level of agreement it wants: its own text seeks a minimum of 51% of stake from DReps to run the process it describes.

The process runs in five stages, beginning with a stage zero of strategic planning whose pieces, a vision and a strategy, have to be approved on chain before an annual budget cycle starts at all. What follows is collection of proposals, review, consolidation into a budget and execution through withdrawals with milestone reporting. The new part is the tightening after the previous cycle: the text says the improvements came out of consultation with DReps, vendors and the wider community, that they align the workflow with constitutional requirements, and that any future change to the framework has to be submitted as a fresh information action reproducing the text with its amendments, so the version in force is always the one on chain.

Voting closed at the start of epoch 623 with 68.4% of counted DRep power behind it, on 138 yes ballots, 32 no and 7 abstain, past the 51% it asked for. Among the twelve largest DReps on its ballot list, ranked by delegated power at epoch 623, nine voted yes, one abstained and two voted no. The pools recorded 23 yes ballots, 1 no and 1 abstain, a yes stake share of 5.95%.

## Why two committee members called it unconstitutional

Five committee members found the framework constitutional and two did not, a reading of 71.43% with all seven voting. It is the only one of the three actions decided in these epochs whose committee tally holds a no vote, and the published objection is worth reading, because it is an argument about what an information action does rather than about the process itself.

The framework says that publishing it ensures every governance participant works from a shared, authoritative reference. The objection takes that at its word: if the process is formalised on chain, DReps will in practice reject proposals that do not follow it, even though the protocol still permits anyone to file a request directly. It cites a precedent from earlier in this series, DReps appealing to the strategy vote when rejecting proposals for not complying with it rather than on their merits, and sets that against the constitution's guarantee that no formal membership is required to take part in Cardano governance. On that reading an endorsement of a facilitated process turns a voluntary route into the only viable one.

Nothing follows from the finding mechanically. An information action is never ratified, so the committee reading has no bar to clear and the ledger does nothing with it either way. The argument is still the sharpest thing in this window.

## The first draw of the new spending period

[Amaru](/ga/2f2fc2539dde550d3c83bea67c11b03861407976bbe8d07c5b0e124aa8a12baa00/) received ₳10.1M at the start of epoch 621, the window's only treasury withdrawal and the first charged against the net change limit for the spending period that began at epoch 613. It had been ratified at the start of epoch 620, before this window, on 70.93% of counted DRep power, a vote the [review of epochs 615 to 620](/governance-review/epochs-615-620/) covers. The proposal describes Amaru as a block producing node designed for low hardware requirements, and calls this its second treasury withdrawal.

After the payment the treasury balance stood ₳6.3M below its epoch 620 reading. It recovered to ₳1.66B by epoch 623.

## ₳50M ratified for Orion

[Cardano x Draper Dragon: Orion Fund](/ga/8807e3cee3be5647742c329c1dea242f0c1c29b8ad050e15169e3b0302fcbfd600/) was ratified at the start of epoch 623 on 74.89% of counted DRep power, 109 yes ballots, 37 no and 10 abstain, with all seven committee members in favour.

It is not a grant and not a loan. The proposal sets up a limited partnership managed by an external general partner, which would invest in companies building on or integrating with Cardano, and it asks the DReps to approve only the first tranche, ₳50M, which it puts at roughly fifteen million dollars at an assumed ada price of thirty cents. The net proceeds of the direct investments made with the majority of that contribution are to be returned to the treasury on a repayment schedule set out in the proposal, and the text describes the treasury's exposure as incremental, capped and governed, meaning each further tranche needs its own vote. What it proposes to return is a plan rather than a promise the ledger can hold. The ₳50M is due one epoch later, in the [review of epochs 624 to 626](/governance-review/epochs-624-626/).

Two more withdrawals reached their voting deadline at the start of epoch 624, the window's closing boundary: [Cardano Defi Liquidity Budget - Withdrawal 1](/ga/e5c4d7ebaad03ac127ec19a286605a188d6fea1c54e075c619ca6993b7fa9fd100/) at 74.13% of counted DRep power as of that close and [Dingo: a Production-Grade Block Producer in Go by Blink Labs](/ga/f35285db3c4e085ad331843b3007737952b8a322bb3216311edc37fdf44ad3da00/) at 70.3%. Two new requests, [Pebble + Gerolamo - HLabs 2026 Budget](/ga/b11527fbcdc9d41e8f497de64a029a18673a5eefc413718459046f0b7a1a665600/) for ₳8.0M and [Cardano Summit 2026 and TOKEN2049 Singapore](/ga/bd91fa7ea9b4e09f76cbde3abb0b564ffc60b27e18af464ccec9bef9c718d08700/) for ₳14.1M, were submitted inside the window and still open at its close.

The window recorded 563 ballots, superseded votes included: 518 from DReps, 19 from committee members and 26 from stake pools. Final ballots came from 166 DReps and 21 pools.
