## The first ada out of the treasury

At the start of epoch 571 the ledger paid ₳1.5M out of the Cardano treasury to [Amaru Treasury Withdrawal 2025](/ga/60ed6ab43c840ff888a8af30a1ed27b41e9f4a91a89822b2b63d1bfc52aeec4500/). It is the first enacted withdrawal this record holds, and after fourteen windows of rules, ceilings and signals it is worth saying plainly what it bought: work on a second program capable of producing Cardano blocks, so the network stops depending on a single implementation of its own software. The ₳1.5M is split across five scopes with an owner each, for the ledger and overall architecture, consensus and simulation testing, ad hoc and middleware work, project management and marketing, and a contingency reserve. The request did not come out of nowhere: its document says it follows a budget proposal of its own that had already been agreed, and that each of the five addresses sits under a contract which can send unspent ada back to the treasury once an agreed expiry date has passed. The document is also candid about timing: the contract the project wanted to use was still being audited, so it went ahead with a simpler, already audited setup because the people it pays were weeks from the end of their payroll.

It was ratified at the start of epoch 570 with 74.53% of counted DRep power behind it, on 198 yes ballots to 6 no, and all seven committee members voting yes, then took effect one epoch later. Every withdrawal follows that pattern: ratified at one boundary, paid at the next.

## Thirty nine requests at once

In epoch 570, 39 further withdrawal requests were filed, ₳275.0M between them. Thirty seven arrived in a single transaction and two on their own, which changes nothing about how they are decided: each is a separate proposal with its own tally, its own committee vote and its own deadline, and DReps could say yes to one and no to the next. What they share is the paperwork. Every request Intersect submitted on behalf of a vendor carries the same account of how it will be run: one of 39 proposals giving effect to the budget agreed in epoch 564, with milestones, acceptance criteria and payment dates fixed in a contract, a third party signing off each milestone, and the ada held in a treasury reserve contract until it is disbursed.

They are the aggregate budget arriving as invoices, and two of the invoices are smaller than the budget line. The 39 come to ₳275.0M against the ₳275,269,340 the budget listed because the exhibitions request was cut from ₳1,119,333 to ₳889,500, which its document says was to avoid overlapping with other proposals before the community, and the research request from ₳26,848,000 to ₳26,840,000. The four largest take most of the total, ₳96.8M for core engineering, ₳69.5M for the community innovation funding programme, ₳26.8M for research and ₳15.8M for the organisation administering the bundle, and the smallest is ₳45,217 for maintaining a set of core tools.

## A ballot count that is not a turnout

Epoch 571 recorded 2,350 DRep ballots, by far the most in the record up to then, and epoch 572 another 1,413. That is not a surge of new participants. A DRep working through all 39 requests casts 39 ballots, and the 2,350 came from 140 distinct DReps. Across the three epochs 200 distinct DReps cast a final ballot, against 838 holding delegated power at epoch 572, the most up to then. The count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 317 to 332.

The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.31B at epoch 570 and ₳5.26B at epoch 572, the ten largest holding 45.33% and 46.58% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳6.84M to ₳1.81B, the highest up to then, because the ₳1.5M paid out was smaller than what flowed in over the same epochs. Of the ₳350M the ceiling allows for the period ending at epoch 604, ₳348.5M was still unused, since a request consumes nothing until it is paid.

## Two governance tools wait

Two budgets for governance software were still being voted on, and neither moves any money by itself: both are info actions, and both documents say a separate withdrawal action would follow a yes. [Cardano GovTool Budget - 12 months full active maintenance and development](/ga/9d213a57684d7ddf6f3350c80d042639ecbed5ccccc4a05bf54959a086593e7b00/), the budget for the software most DReps vote through, was one epoch from its deadline at the close. Its document asks for ₳1.15M for twelve months of maintenance and development, says the funding the tool had was about to run out, and describes the ask as a leaner version of an earlier proposal, cut after weeks of consultation into tiers from keeping the tool online to full development of every part of it. [Tempo for Cardono Governance - Maintenance & Development Budget for 2025](/ga/495bd3cd44ea6bc0b4c2dbb1e4cdc7e214874c2c07490a4e2476a627ef91128000/), for an independent voting application built for phones, had four epochs left. It asks for ₳380,000 over six months, ₳200,000 of it to keep running its own node and indexer rather than rely on a third party for its data, and ₳180,000 for development.
