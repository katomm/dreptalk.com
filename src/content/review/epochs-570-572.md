## The first ada out of the treasury

At the start of epoch 571 the ledger paid ₳1.5M out of the Cardano treasury to [Amaru Treasury Withdrawal 2025](/ga/60ed6ab43c840ff888a8af30a1ed27b41e9f4a91a89822b2b63d1bfc52aeec4500/). It is the first treasury withdrawal ever enacted on Cardano, and after fourteen windows of rules, ceilings and signals it is worth saying plainly what it bought: work on a second program capable of producing Cardano blocks, so the network stops depending on a single implementation of its own software. The ₳1.5M is split across five scopes with an owner each, for the ledger and overall architecture, consensus and simulation testing, ad hoc and middleware work, project management and marketing, and a contingency reserve. Its document says it follows a budget proposal of its own that had already been agreed, that each of the five addresses sits under a contract which can send unspent ada back to the treasury once an agreed expiry date has passed, and that the contract the project wanted to use was still being audited, so it went ahead with a simpler, already audited setup because the people it pays were weeks from the end of their payroll.

It was ratified at the start of epoch 570 with 74.53% of DRep power behind it, on 198 yes ballots to 6 no and 11 abstain, and all seven committee members voting yes, then took effect one epoch later. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. Every withdrawal follows that pattern: ratified at one boundary, paid at the next.

The largest DReps wrote about the second implementation rather than the money. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) voted yes because a second node reduces reliance on one implementation and because the intermediary contracts and on chain metadata keep the transactions "clear, trackable, and easily auditable". [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) wrote that the request corresponds directly to the roadmap the DReps had approved and that the cost breakdown "appears efficient". [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) called it "a groundbreaking milestone" and noted the independent auditor. [SASA_nagamaru_ながまる](/dreps/drep1ytuddrgqca0xd4aams6pl9zndpcmxg0rs7z3mggjelm6kscp4arg5/) described what the association behind it, PRAGMA, had already published: responsibilities, code and financial structures shared in the open. The objections were about pay and about measurement. TriangleForces voted no because a full time equivalent valued at 200,000 dollars a year "risks alienating the community by projecting an image of excess". Agora, the DRep run by Rodrigo Pacini, abstained because the proposal has no quantitative targets, "such as test coverage percentage, SPO adoption, or blocks produced".

## Thirty nine requests at once

In epoch 570, 39 further withdrawal requests were filed, ₳275.0M between them. Thirty seven arrived in a single transaction and two on their own, which changes nothing about how they are decided: each is a separate proposal with its own tally, its own committee vote and its own deadline, and DReps could say yes to one and no to the next. What they share is the paperwork. Every request Intersect submitted on behalf of a vendor carries the same account of how it will be run: one of 39 proposals giving effect to the budget agreed in epoch 564, with milestones, acceptance criteria and payment dates fixed in a contract, a third party signing off each milestone, and the ada held in a treasury reserve contract until it is disbursed.

They are the aggregate budget arriving as invoices, and two of the invoices are smaller than the budget line. The 39 come to ₳275.0M against the ₳275,269,340 the budget listed because the exhibitions request was cut from ₳1,119,333 to ₳889,500, which its document says was to avoid overlapping with other proposals before the community, and the research request from ₳26,848,000 to ₳26,840,000. The four largest take most of the total, ₳96.8M for Input Output Engineering, ₳69.5M for Project Catalyst, the community innovation funding programme, ₳26.8M for Input Output Research and ₳15.8M for Intersect, the organisation administering the bundle, and the smallest is ₳45,217 for maintaining a set of core tools.

## The first ballots on the largest request

The largest of the 39 is [the core engineering request](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e01/), ₳96.8M, and the ballots cast on it in epochs 571 and 572 already show the argument. No share is given here, because the request was decided after this window. Yoroi voted yes in epoch 571, calling it "a strategic investment" in protocol upgrades and scaling layers, reviewed by the technical steering committee. EMURGO voted yes in the same epoch, "a structural and long-term investment". YUTA voted yes and attached a request for next time: bundled proposals should get "a temperature check vote for each item".

The [Cardano Foundation](/dreps/drep1ydpfkyjxzeqvalf6fgvj7lznrk8kcmfnvy9hyl6gr6ez6wgsjaelx/) abstained in epoch 572, in one document that sets out its ballot on all 39 requests. On this one it was "not confident that the current version offers sufficient transparency or accountability", because scope, cost effectiveness and safeguards had not been adjusted after community feedback. The same document abstains on the ₳26.8M research request as "materially too high" and votes yes on the ₳69.5M community funding request "to ensure continuity for the main source of grant funding" in a transition year. Two smaller no votes went further: Maureen wrote that ₳96.8M reads like "a university research department's wishlist" and that the proposal should "pick three things and do them excellently", and a DRep without a published name that the technical steering committee's alternative had been much more mature and that assigning everything to one company is unsustainable.

On [the community funding request](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e17/), ₳69.5M, Yoroi voted yes in epoch 571 as "a critical driver" of decentralised funding, InputEndorsers abstained because it had been a paid reviewer of the programme for years and did not want to be "a tipping vote", and No stress voted no, preferring "less than 20 million per round and less than 3 rounds per year".

## A ballot count that is not a turnout

Epoch 571 recorded 2,350 DRep ballots, by far the most in the record up to then, and epoch 572 another 1,413. That is not a surge of new participants. A DRep working through all 39 requests casts 39 ballots, and the 2,350 came from 140 distinct DReps. Across the three epochs 200 distinct DReps cast a final ballot, against 838 holding delegated power at epoch 572, the most up to then. The count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 317 to 332.

The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.31B at epoch 570 and ₳5.26B at epoch 572, the ten largest holding 45.33% and 46.58% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳6.84M to ₳1.81B, the highest up to then, because the ₳1.5M paid out was smaller than what flowed in over the same epochs. Of the ₳350M the ceiling allows for the period ending at epoch 604, ₳348.5M was still unused, since a request consumes nothing until it is paid.

## Two governance tools wait

Two budgets for governance software were still being voted on, and neither moves any money by itself: both are info actions, and both documents say a separate withdrawal action would follow a yes. [Cardano GovTool Budget - 12 months full active maintenance and development](/ga/9d213a57684d7ddf6f3350c80d042639ecbed5ccccc4a05bf54959a086593e7b00/), the budget for the governance software used to register as a DRep, delegate, submit proposals and vote, was one epoch from its deadline at the close. Its document asks for ₳1.15M for twelve months of maintenance and development, says the funding the tool had was about to run out, and describes the ask as a leaner version of an earlier proposal, cut after weeks of consultation into tiers from keeping the tool online to full development of every part of it. The ballots cast on it in these epochs split on the price. SASA voted yes in epoch 570 because the tool is "the primary interface for decentralized governance on Cardano and a critical public good", and the Cardano Foundation in epoch 572, calling its yes "a pragmatic decision" to keep the one non commercial tool viable. Dave voted no in epoch 572 because the tool's "current unreliability impedes effective governance participation", and Patrick_Tobler in epoch 571 because "the requested funds are too high".

[Tempo for Cardono Governance - Maintenance & Development Budget for 2025](/ga/495bd3cd44ea6bc0b4c2dbb1e4cdc7e214874c2c07490a4e2476a627ef91128000/), for an independent voting application built for phones, had three epochs of voting left, with its deadline at the start of epoch 576. It asks for ₳380,000 over six months, ₳200,000 of it to keep running its own node and indexer rather than rely on a third party for its data, and ₳180,000 for development. The [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/) has the first of the two.
