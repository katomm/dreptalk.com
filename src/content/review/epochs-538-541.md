## Ratified at one boundary, after months of workshops

[Cardano Constitution to Replace the Interim Constitution](/ga/8c653ee5c9800e6d31e79b5a7f7d4400c81d44717ad4db633dc18d4c07e4a4fd00/) was ratified at the start of epoch 541. Its stored tally records 385 DRep yes ballots, 22 no and 6 abstain, 84.9% of DRep power against a 75% bar, with ₳3.06B of power for it and ₳0.08B against. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. All seven committee members voted yes. Stake pools have no vote on a constitution at all.

The document itself was not written in those four epochs. Its own text records 63 workshops held in 52 countries between July and October 2024, with more than 1,400 participants, 63 voting delegates and 63 alternates elected at those workshops, and the 63 delegates approving the final text at a convention in December 2024 with 95% voting in favour. The interim constitution had built its own replacement into itself: it was meant to be temporary, carried only the minimum the first upgrade needed, and required a successor to be submitted on chain no later than 31 January 2025. This proposal went on chain on 30 January, with a day to spare.

The new text carries the rules, parameters and guardrails the interim document had already set out, together with the values the workshops agreed on and a process for amending the text later. The treasury tax proposal in the next section shows why the guardrails matter: filed in epoch 539, while the constitution was still on the ballot, it asked for the lowest treasury cut that both the interim and the new text permit, 10%, and cites both documents.

## The faults were noted, and it passed anyway

The published yes rationales mostly said the same two things: the text has faults, and passing it beats waiting. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/), the largest, wrote that it "prevents arbitrary interpretation" and that rejecting it "risks costly reconstitution". The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) called it "a living document". Pi Lanningham listed "material faults that should be addressed in a future revision" and voted yes because "we could iterate on this forever and never reach consensus". HOSKY voted yes for the delegates who had made the compromises in Argentina, with disagreements of its own. Rodrigo, who had sat in the workshops in Buenos Aires and Santiago, drew on the constitution of their home country: the search for a perfect one had left people "stuck with the old one". Socious reported its own internal vote, six yes and four abstentions out of ten. AKYO singled out one new sentence, a written maximum supply of 45,000,000,000 ada. The DRep of Hephaestus Stake Pool voted yes with reservations about the "shepherding" of the workshop conversations, and Cerkoryn noted that the interim text might require this draft to pass before any other could be proposed.

The no votes were few and specific. The Tempo DRep found no adequate guardrails against misuse of the treasury. HeptaSean wrote that proposals for "a leaner and less ambiguous constitution" had been sidelined at the workshops and the convention, with only minor corrections allowed. One DRep objected to a technology based constitution resting on "subjective interpretation of what is 'reasonable'", and Cardano Advocate to the one token one vote model at its base, which it called exclusive for newcomers. Between them the 22 no ballots carried ₳0.08B of delegated power.

The committee's seven yes votes read as verification rather than opinion. The Intersect Constitutional Council wrote that it had compared the hashes of the text on chain and the text approved in Buenos Aires and Nairobi and found them identical, cross referenced against photographs of the convention. Input Output went through the six ratification criteria the interim constitution's appendix sets out and recorded how each was met. The Cardano Atlantic Council traced the same appendix from consultation to the conventions, where 95% of the delegates approved the text.

The vote itself ran in two parts. DRep support arrived early, 142 yes ballots in the proposal's first epoch carrying ₳1,409.6M, and kept coming. The committee voted later: three members yes in epoch 539, the remaining four in epoch 540, and ratification followed at the next boundary. Which of the two bars was cleared last cannot be read off the record, because no DRep share is reconstructed for the earlier boundaries. The constitution took effect one epoch after ratification, in the [review of epochs 542 to 544](/governance-review/epochs-542-544/).

## Who gets the reward pot

[Decrease Treasury Tax from 20% to 10%](/ga/941502b0aa104c850d197923259444d2b57cab7af18b63143775465aaacc84f500/), submitted in epoch 539, is about money rather than rules. Every epoch Cardano mints a pot of rewards out of its reserves and adds the transaction fees to it. Before any of it reaches the people who stake their ada, the treasury takes a fifth off the top, and that fifth is what funds every proposal asking for a payout. The proposal wanted to make it a tenth.

Its argument is that the share was set at the launch of Shelley and never looked at again, that halving it would raise staking rewards by about 12.5%, roughly from 4.0% to 4.5% a year, and that the constitution ratified in the same window permits anything between 10% and 30%, which puts the tenth it asks for exactly at the bottom of the range. It also cites an earlier update of the Plutus cost model as a precedent for changing a parameter through this process. As an economic parameter the change needed 67% of counted DRep power and two thirds of the committee, with no pool vote.

The first ballots argued about what the treasury is for, and no share is given here. The 120 ballots it drew in epoch 540 were 49 yes, 69 no and 2 abstain, and the no ballots that epoch carried ₳813.1M of DRep power against ₳312.0M behind the yes. YUTA voted no that epoch because "a lot of fundamental research and development" still has to be paid for, and [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) because the scalability era "is not complete" and a freed share would be better used in decentralised finance than paid out. AdaStat voted no because rewards should grow through more projects and more fees. On the other side, MUEN voted yes in epoch 540 because the price of ada had risen about 2.3 to 2.5 times since November 2024 while rewards keep shrinking, and Cerkoryn in epoch 541 because researchers had first recommended 5% before launch and the effective rate is about 33% once rewards are calculated on total rather than active stake. It was still open at the close of epoch 541 with a deadline at the start of epoch 546, and the [review of epochs 545 to 547](/governance-review/epochs-545-547/) has the result.

## Participation catches up with delegation

Epoch 540 was the busiest epoch for DReps up to then, 187 ballots across the two proposals, and the count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 251 at epoch 538 to 363 at epoch 541, the most up to then. 296 distinct DReps cast a final ballot in the window, out of the 766 holding power. The power delegated to them grew ₳402.1M to ₳3.64B, the highest reading up to then, while the ten largest held a slightly smaller slice of it, 43.87% at epoch 538 and 43.23% at epoch 541. The treasury reading rose ₳13.2M to ₳1.68B, with nothing paid out of it yet.
