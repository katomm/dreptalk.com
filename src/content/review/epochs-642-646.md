## The treasury pays ₳44.2M, and seven requests run out

The treasury paid twelve withdrawals across these five epochs, ₳44.2M together, and seven others reached the end of their voting windows with nothing. A twentieth was ratified inside the window for payment after it, so nineteen were either paid or refused here. The bar for a withdrawal is 67% of counted DRep power. The closest of the seven that ran out stood at 63.0%, and the lowest share behind a payment was 68.4%.

More than half the money went into a single withdrawal, ₳25.4M to Intersect at 75.7%, for a year of its operations from June 2026 to June 2027: the member organisation, technical stewardship of the core repositories, incident response and release coordination, at a dollar figure its text puts below the previous year's ask. Most of the rest is upkeep rather than new building: five requests of a few hundred thousand ada each keep a set of widely used data and indexing tools running, one funds the maintenance of hardware wallet support, and one covers a set of core developer tools. None of that produces anything a user would notice, and all of it stops working if nobody pays for it.

Three of the twelve fund named pieces of protocol work. [Withdraw 3,810,423 ada for Mithril Protocol](/ga/b3d452bff7769d7f557ec6b8974760ee6c5e496c276652b654032966621e0ccf05/), for the certificate scheme that lets a node bootstrap without replaying the whole chain, had the highest DRep yes share of the twelve at 79.3%. [IO: Hydra](/ga/4f6e5df9a6e14bd49e406e2bbb0d108b3be2be64b9569b930053a0c1eebe0d4700/), for the layer that settles batches of transactions off the main chain, passed at 74.3%, and [Eternl: Path to Sustainability - v2](/ga/fbb8d1a4a8d6b62f8cd706944a0582b884c2b90187b8fada7953d5c6a33eb5a700/), the resubmitted wallet request of the [review of epochs 636 to 638](/governance-review/epochs-636-638/), at 71.6%.

The seven that ran out have less in common than a budget round suggests. The largest was [Cardano Builder DAO](/ga/4b7abfa70d98858750c50cd651d54c2955303b5410b34f40eb259f2fffc7c93900/), ₳20M at 11.0% of counted power for a fund that would allocate money to builders against usage targets, and next to it [Alchemy](/ga/0f75dd11be0b7a6cb4b305a175b17cf4b60cd307c052fccabf9f572950e7058300/) at ₳10M and 5.3%, half of it launch liquidity for bitcoin backed products with returns due back to the treasury each quarter. [Blockfrost](/ga/5439b6141625436ccf600f910bb0b3301b6288933a2cdf7939758848ae8b999700/) asked for ₳9.8M to turn itself into a community owned not-for-profit over eighteen months, not the request the [review of epochs 633 to 635](/governance-review/epochs-633-635/) saw run out, and reached 43.3%. A request for ₳4.0M to bring real world payments to the chain, a payment card drawing on balances held on chain, reached 54.4% on 68 yes ballots against 61. [Strike Finance Liquidity Deployment](/ga/8721696358acdd43e34e5ed9ef1b3e2a1d2af9c1aa1972e017b9b9271b7ddc7000/) asked for ₳9M as treasury capital to be sold into a stablecoin and deployed for twelve months rather than as a grant, and had the tightest head count of the whole window, 65 yes against 64 no, while still reading as 20.5% of power. A head count that close next to a share that far from the bar is the weighting at work: each ballot carries its delegated power.

The smallest of the seven was ₳103,000 to reimburse a ₳100,000 deposit lost in 2024 to a node bug on one of the first info actions, plus ₳3,000 for the staking rewards forgone, and at 63.0% it is the one that came nearest to passing. An info action closed at the same boundary, to epoch 643: [Reforming Treasury Governance](/ga/5d3a1f657fe330b51b459ca9b482fc1a3b6e3336227f880d8a058771c23e3ce200/) put up for discussion a thematic budget in place of withdrawals voted one against another, and drew 7.1% of counted DRep power.

## A lower minimum for the committee

The minimum size of the Constitutional Committee fell from seven members to five at the start of epoch 643. [Reduce the committeeMinSize parameter from 7 to 5](/ga/c75bb221606687aa858ec89c7a15c88e9c17054f2e045ae31ecc8a9687cd206e00/) had been ratified one epoch earlier with 80.1% of counted DRep power behind it, on 137 yes votes, 22 no and 17 abstain, against a 75% bar, and six committee members voted for it with none against.

This matters because of what happened eight windows earlier. A single resignation left six members where the protocol required seven, and everything needing a committee ruling stopped until the seat was filled, which the [edition on that resignation](/governance-review/epochs-597-599/) describes. A proposal to cut the minimum to five followed within a few windows and was invalidated by a collision with another parameter change live at the same time. The resubmission tied its own ratification to the cost model change of the [review of epochs 636 to 638](/governance-review/epochs-636-638/), enacted in epoch 638, and carried at the second attempt.

The minimum decides when the committee stops being able to hold up its end. With fewer seats filled than the minimum, an action that needs a committee vote cannot be ratified at all, whatever the DReps and the pools do. A motion of no confidence and a change to the committee itself stay possible, because those are the two the committee does not vote on. Seven members were active at the end of epoch 646, exactly the old minimum and two above the new one. What changed is how many seats can fall vacant before the rest of the agenda stops. Its text notes the other side of that: at five members, four yes votes reach the two thirds bar and two no votes block, where at seven it takes five and three.

## The count of voting DReps keeps falling

The count of DReps that had cast a ballot in the last 12 epochs fell from 341 at epoch 642 to 318 at epoch 646, and the power delegated to DReps fell by ₳115.7M over the same stretch, the always abstain and always no confidence options left out. Across the five epochs 2,553 ballots were cast across all three roles, superseded votes included, and 229 DReps cast a final ballot, each counted once.

## The hard fork clears the pool bar by 1.4 points

[Hard Fork to Protocol Version 11 ('van Rossem' Hard Fork)](/ga/fdd468da5cc4ac8431dcd7e2b3211666c73bc229f85879469f67f1d9d51d344d00/) was ratified at the boundary to epoch 643 and took effect one epoch later. On the DRep side the ballots were overwhelmingly for it, 180 yes against a single no and 4 abstain, 77.6% of counted power against a 60% bar.

What the upgrade delivers is the set of Plutus operations the cost model change of the previous window had already priced. Those operations were written into the price list first and become callable only when the protocol version rises, which is what this hard fork does. The [review of epochs 636 to 638](/governance-review/epochs-636-638/) covers the pricing half of the pair.

Its text adds that all built-in functions become available in all three Plutus versions rather than only the newest, and records the constitutional condition that at least 85% of stake pools by active stake had to be running a supporting node before ratification, a readiness check separate from the vote.

The pool side was the tight one. 383 pools voted yes, none voted no, 48 abstained, and the reading was still 52.4% of stake against a 51% bar. On a hard fork every pool that does not vote counts as no, and unlike other action types nothing a pool's reward account delegates can change that. The upgrade went through with 1.4 points to spare.
