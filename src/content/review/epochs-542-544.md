## The rules governing the next proposals

[Cardano Constitution to Replace the Interim Constitution](/ga/8c653ee5c9800e6d31e79b5a7f7d4400c81d44717ad4db633dc18d4c07e4a4fd00/) took effect at the start of epoch 542, one epoch after the ratification the [review of epochs 538 to 541](/governance-review/epochs-538-541/) covers. The DReps who backed it did not call it finished. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) called it "a living document", [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) wrote that it "prevents arbitrary interpretation" and that rejecting it "risks costly reconstitution", and Pi Lanningham voted yes while listing "material faults that should be addressed in a future revision", because "we could iterate on this forever and never reach consensus". HOSKY voted yes on the ground that it reflects the will of the delegates at the convention in Argentina, disagreements included, and Hephaestus Stake Pool with reservations about how the conversations had been steered. The Tempo DRep voted no because the text carries no adequate guardrails against misuse of the treasury.

Two of its provisions decide what can happen next. The first is the set of written ranges for the protocol parameters governance is allowed to touch, which the interim constitution already carried and the new text keeps. A proposal may still ask for a change, but if the value it asks for falls outside the range, it is asking for something the constitution does not permit: the share of rewards the treasury takes, for instance, has to stay between 10% and 30%, which puts a floor under the proposal to halve it that was open in the same window. The second is a clock. The committee sitting at this point had been appointed for the trial phase, its term runs out in September 2025, at epoch 580, and after that the community elects its successor. Both provisions decide the shape of most of the editions that follow.

## The first proposal filed under the new rules

[Defining the Cardano Vision and Roadmap for 2025 and beyond](/ga/56f39054758f1a3cedc1de9225d66bf270b62dfdbfbc5399f1d6d43aceffc63600/), submitted in epoch 542, asked DReps to say whether they agreed with a development plan rather than to approve anything. The plan came from the product committee of Intersect, assembled out of community survey results, and its priorities are the concrete part: scaling the main chain with parallel block production, the protocol called Leios, pulling liquidity in from other chains, building out the second layer, giving developers better tools to work with, programmable assets, and keeping several independent implementations of the node itself. A DRep voting on it was being asked to endorse that order of priorities, and the document says what the endorsement was for: the result would inform the budget proposals Intersect was preparing, including the budget conversations already under way. It also makes the proposal a moving target by design. The roadmap would be revised while the vote ran, in response to DRep feedback, DReps were asked to attach their reasons to their ballots for the product committee to read, and to vote again as the text changed. A yes meant support for the roadmap, a no meant a lack of it, and an abstention neither. So the superseded ballots the counts below include are, on this proposal, part of what it asked for.

It drew 87 yes ballots across the roles in its first three epochs, against 4 no and 6 abstain. Its deadline was the start of epoch 549, and no share is given here. The reasons attached did what the document asked for. YUTA voted yes in epoch 543 while saying it "may change my vote" after hearing "honest opposing opinions from developers and researchers", and named the items it had heard doubts about. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) voted yes with the objection that scalability "is addressed from three sides, which might be unnecessary", and suggested focusing on two. [Martin Lang](/dreps/drep1ytwmwvtd0a8lr45ssner2tjxzv5y8q03w3606yeald9mdmgmwecja/) found the roadmap solid, asked that work on several nodes not be overdone, and missed a mention of post quantum research.

## The first reasons on the treasury tax

[Decrease Treasury Tax from 20% to 10%](/ga/941502b0aa104c850d197923259444d2b57cab7af18b63143775465aaacc84f500/), filed in epoch 539, was open through the whole window with its deadline at the start of epoch 546, and the ballots already cast on it disagree about what the treasury is for. No share is given here. YUTA voted no in epoch 540 because "a lot of fundamental research and development" still has to be paid for, listing scalability, partner chains, alternative nodes and governance tools. CardanoYoda voted no the same day: the scalability era "is not complete", and an extra 6 ada per 100,000 staked makes no sense if the price of ada falls. AdaStat voted no because rewards should grow through more projects and more fees, and Socious because the proposal "relies on the uncertain assumption" that the price of ada will rise.

The yes side argued that the treasury is big enough. [CryptoCrow](/dreps/drep1ytfwpmt2fvdnyvlswyjyggpf8alwkpm7ha6cua9nsak525ssmvm57/) voted yes in epoch 542 because "we have a substantial treasury already" and higher rewards would help pools through a bear market. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) put the gain at approximately 12.5% more in staking rewards and called the 20% rate arbitrary and never reassessed. Cerkoryn wrote that researchers had first recommended 5% before launch and that the effective rate is about 33%, because rewards are calculated on total rather than active stake. Martin Lang voted yes in epoch 544 on the ground that the high rate had done its job of building the treasury and that a lower one "put pressure on the expenses that are planned for the next year". The [review of epochs 545 to 547](/governance-review/epochs-545-547/) has the result and the committee's reasons.

## The ten largest hold less, and the spread gets wider

The power delegated to DReps rose from ₳3.71B at epoch 542 to ₳3.88B at epoch 544, ₳169.5M more and the highest reading up to then, held by 792 DReps. The ten largest held 42.94% of it at epoch 544, their smallest share up to then and well below the 50.16% of epoch 522.

Read alone, that looks like power spreading out. The other measure of the same spread says the opposite: the Gini figure, 0.9197 at epoch 544, was the highest up to then, which means delegated power was more unevenly distributed across the DReps holding it than ever. Both can be true at once, because they ask different questions: the ten biggest held a smaller share, while inequality across all the DReps holding delegated power increased. The two measures alone do not show where in the distribution that happened, and neither says which delegations moved.

Over the three epochs DReps cast 192 ballots, committee members 3 and pools 3, superseded votes included, with final ballots from 155 distinct DReps. The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 368 to 386, the most up to then. The treasury reading gained ₳8.7M to ₳1.69B, with nothing paid out of it, and the [review of epochs 545 to 547](/governance-review/epochs-545-547/) has the decision on the treasury tax and the arrival of two competing spending limits.
