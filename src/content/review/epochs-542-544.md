## Two rules that now bind

[Cardano Constitution to Replace the Interim Constitution](/ga/8c653ee5c9800e6d31e79b5a7f7d4400c81d44717ad4db633dc18d4c07e4a4fd00/) took effect at the start of epoch 542, one epoch after the ratification the [review of epochs 538 to 541](/governance-review/epochs-538-541/) covers.

Two of its provisions change what can happen next. The first is a set of written ranges for the protocol parameters governance is allowed to touch. A proposal may still ask for a change, but if the value it asks for falls outside the range, it is asking for something the constitution does not permit: the share of rewards the treasury takes, for instance, has to stay between 10% and 30%, which puts a floor under the proposal to halve it that was open in the same window. The second is a clock. The committee sitting at this point had been appointed for the trial phase, its term runs out in September 2025, at epoch 580, and after that the community elects its successor. Both provisions decide the shape of most of the editions that follow.

## The first proposal filed under the new rules

[Defining the Cardano Vision and Roadmap for 2025 and beyond](/ga/56f39054758f1a3cedc1de9225d66bf270b62dfdbfbc5399f1d6d43aceffc63600/), submitted in epoch 542, asked DReps to say whether they agreed with a development plan rather than to approve anything. The plan came from the product committee of Intersect, assembled out of community survey results, and its priorities are the concrete part: scaling the main chain with parallel block production, pulling liquidity in from other chains, building out the second layer, giving developers better tools to work with, programmable assets, and keeping several independent implementations of the node itself. A DRep voting on it was being asked to endorse that order of priorities, with the budget discussions of the coming epochs in view.

It drew 8 yes ballots across the roles in epoch 542, then 46 yes, 3 no and 4 abstain in epoch 543 and 33 yes, 1 no and 2 abstain in epoch 544, 87 yes to 4 no and 6 abstain in all, with the DRep yes ballots of epoch 543 amounting to ₳782.8M at that epoch's readings. Its deadline was the start of epoch 549.

## The ten largest hold less, and the spread gets wider

The power delegated to DReps rose from ₳3.71B at epoch 542 to ₳3.88B at epoch 544, ₳169.5M more and the highest reading up to then, held by 792 DReps. The ten largest held 42.94% of it at epoch 544, their smallest share up to then and well below the 50.16% of epoch 522.

Read alone, that looks like power spreading out. The other measure of the same spread says the opposite: the Gini figure, 0.9197 at epoch 544, was the highest up to then, which means delegated power was more unevenly distributed across the DReps holding it than ever. Both can be true at once, because they ask different questions. The ten biggest hold a smaller slice of a much larger cake, while the hundreds of DReps below them hold amounts so different from one another that the overall spread keeps widening. Neither figure says which delegations moved.

Over the three epochs DReps cast 192 ballots, committee members 3 and pools 3, superseded votes included, with final ballots from 155 distinct DReps. The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 368 to 386, the most up to then. The treasury reading gained ₳8.7M to ₳1.69B, with nothing paid out of it, and the [review of epochs 545 to 547](/governance-review/epochs-545-547/) has the first decision on what may leave it.
