## Plomin, the hard fork that would give DReps a vote

[Hard Fork to Protocol Version 10 ("Plomin" Hard Fork)](/ga/0b19476e40bbbb5e1e8ce153523762e2b6859e7ecacbaf06eae0ee6a447e79b900/), submitted in epoch 529, was the only proposal open in these six epochs, and it was the one that mattered most so far. Everything the series has covered until now happened under a trial arrangement: DReps could register and collect delegated power, but the proposals they could actually decide were limited to opinions. This upgrade would switch on the full set of governance actions, so that a treasury payment, a change to the protocol or the next hard fork would need the DReps as well.

DReps had no vote on it. A hard fork in that phase needed a committee vote and enough of the stake behind the pools, and DRep votes were not part of the requirement. The proposal's own document says what it would switch on and what had to be true before it could be ratified. All seven kinds of governance action would be enabled, with DReps voting on every one of them, and staking rewards would keep accruing but could only be withdrawn once the holder had delegated to a DRep or to one of the predefined options. Before ratification, more than 90 days had to have passed since the Chang upgrade and at least 85% of stake pools by stake had to be running a node able to support the new version, conditions the interim committee and the pools were to verify with readiness reports from the working group inside Intersect that prepares hard forks. Six epochs of pool ballots were therefore also six epochs of a readiness check. So the pools voted: 622 ballots across the six epochs with superseded ballots included, the largest batch of them, 193, in the final epoch, and two committee members added yes ballots in epoch 534. Across both roles 584 of those ballots were yes, 5 no and 35 abstain, and 32 of the abstentions arrived in the final epoch.

Two pools explained why they voted yes despite a worry, and the worry was the DReps. One pool wrote in epoch 534 that it had significant concerns about the share of ada delegated to DReps, 7.5% against 58% delegated to pools, and about how that delegation was spread, and voted yes because the upgrade was necessary all the same. Another wrote in epoch 533 that it had reservations about the delegation rate and the readiness of the ecosystem, but that "continued hesitation will not get us anywhere". A third, run by a member of the interim committee, abstained and said why: it would keep its pool out of every vote and park its wallets on the automatic abstain option for as long as its operator sat on the committee. Voting ran to the start of epoch 536, and the [review of epochs 535 to 537](/governance-review/epochs-535-537/) has the result and the switch itself.

## Why the upgrade is called Plomin

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) closed at the start of epoch 529 with 76.54% of DRep power behind it, on stored DRep counts of 209 yes, 9 no and 8 abstain, ₳1.71B of power for it and ₳3.2M against. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. It was filed by the hard fork working group of Intersect and asked for the upgrade to be named in memory of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang. Six committee members voted yes and one abstained, and all 95 recorded pool ballots were yes, a yes stake share the record puts at 7.17%.

The DReps who voted yes wrote about the man. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) wrote that he "embodied the values and ideals of the community, consistently supporting others". Cerkoryn called him "one of the greatest visionaries" it had known. Smaller DReps recalled that he had founded the first fully dollar backed stablecoin on Cardano and had come to blockchain development from traditional finance. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) voted yes because the name had "widespread support" and keeping the old one would cause more confusion, while noting for the future that a name should not be changed just before a hard fork, having already fielded questions about the difference. The one published abstention, from Wada DRep, made the same practical point the other way: the old name "has already traveled", and a later hard fork would be a better dedication. The Eastern Cardano Council abstained as a committee member, citing its position that the committee should not rule on information actions that do not measure sentiment for a future on chain action. An information action of this kind never enacts anything, so the name held because the people who write the release notes chose to honour the signal, not because the ledger made them. The [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/) covers its last epochs of voting.

## Delegation keeps growing for a role that has not started

DReps held ₳2.72B at the epoch 534 reading, ₳417.0M more than at epoch 529, spread across 657 of them, up from 599. Both were the highest readings up to then, and against the ₳134.3M of epoch 508 in the [review of epochs 508 to 513](/governance-review/epochs-508-513/) the delegated total had grown twentyfold in 26 epochs. None of that power could be used on the only proposal open.

The number of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs fell from 198 to 180, which with no DRep vote open was arithmetic rather than a retreat: that count only looks back twelve epochs, so old ballots drop out of it whether or not anyone loses interest. Concentration rose a little, the ten largest DReps holding 44.73% of the power at epoch 529 and 46.47% at epoch 534, and the Gini measure of how unevenly power is spread among the DReps that hold any reached 0.9173, the highest up to then. The treasury gained ₳22.4M between the two readings to ₳1.65B, with no withdrawal paid out of it, because none could yet be proposed. Of the 624 ballots in the window, 622 came from pools, standing behind 619 distinct pools, and 2 from committee members.
