## Intersect files the Plomin hard fork

[Hard Fork to Protocol Version 10 ("Plomin" Hard Fork)](/ga/0b19476e40bbbb5e1e8ce153523762e2b6859e7ecacbaf06eae0ee6a447e79b900/) was the only proposal open in these six epochs. Intersect filed it in epoch 529, after its Hard Fork Working Group recommended the submission and its Technical Steering Committee approved it. Its document says the upgrade would enable all seven kinds of governance action with DRep voting on each, and that staking rewards would keep accruing but could only be withdrawn once the holder had delegated to a DRep or to a predefined option. It also says that more than 90 days would pass after the Chang hard fork before ratification and that at least 85% of pools by stake should have upgraded to a node supporting the new protocol version by then, conditions for the interim committee and the pools to verify with readiness reports from the Hard Fork Working Group.

Under the rules of the bootstrap phase then in force, a hard fork needed a committee vote and a sufficient pool vote, and DRep votes were part of neither requirement. No DRep ballot was recorded in any of the six epochs.

## 581 pools vote yes on Plomin

By the close of epoch 534, 619 pools had cast a final ballot on the hard fork, 581 yes, 4 no and 34 abstain, out of 622 pool ballots with superseded ones included. Input Output and the Cardano Japan Council found it constitutional in epoch 534, the only committee ballots cast on it by then. Voting ran until the start of [epoch 536](/governance-review/epochs-535-537/).

Hazelpool voted yes in epoch 534 while writing that it had "significant concerns" about the share of ada delegated to DReps, 7.5% against 58% delegated to pools, and about how that delegation was distributed. It wrote that it had discussed delaying the hard fork over those concerns, but that control had to rest with all ada holders and not only the interim committee. The AKYO pool voted yes in epoch 533 despite reservations about the delegation rate to DReps and the readiness of the ecosystem, because "continued hesitation will not get us anywhere". The ABLE pool abstained in epoch 531. Its operator wrote that the pool would abstain, and the operator's personal and pool wallets would be delegated to the automatic abstain option, for as long as the operator sat on the constitutional committee.

## The Plomin rename signal closes at 76.5%

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) closed at the start of epoch 529 with 76.5% of DRep power behind it, on 209 yes ballots to 9 no and 8 abstain, ₳1.71B of power voting yes against ₳3.2M voting no. Intersect filed it for its Hard Fork Working Group to name the upgrade in memory of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, as the previous one was named after Phil Chang. Six committee members voted yes and the Eastern Cardano Council abstained. The [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/) quotes the reasons DReps gave.

## Delegation to DReps rises ₳417.0M to ₳2.72B

At the epoch 534 reading, 657 DReps held ₳2.72B of delegated power, ₳417.0M more than at epoch 529, when 599 DReps held ₳2.30B. The total and the number of DReps holding power were both the highest since on-chain governance began.

The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs fell from 198 at epoch 529 to 180 at epoch 534, as older ballots dropped out of it and no proposal open to a DRep vote added new ones. The ten largest DReps held 44.7% of the power at epoch 529 and 46.5% at epoch 534, and the Gini coefficient of that power among the DReps holding any rose to 0.9173, the highest since on-chain governance began. The treasury reading rose ₳22.4M to ₳1.65B, with no withdrawal paid, since treasury withdrawals could not yet be proposed in the bootstrap phase.
