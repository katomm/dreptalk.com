## A second node is the one thing DReps agreed on

[Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget: Amaru Node Development 2025](/ga/bd488931f792651fefa9c6fda185a2c6cec83245b51d994e33090ce36e29cc2600/) closed at the start of epoch 563 with 80.37% of counted DRep power behind it, on 232 yes ballots to 14 no and 10 abstain, ₳4.32B of power for it against ₳28.7M. Six committee members voted yes and one abstained, and all 32 recorded pool ballots were yes.

What it asks for is ₳1.5M towards Amaru, a second program capable of producing Cardano blocks, written mainly in Rust and built under a not for profit association of several Cardano companies rather than by one of them. Cardano had run on a single implementation since launch, and the proposal gives five reasons to change that: lower resource use from different design trade offs, monitoring and tracing that follow industry standards rather than a bespoke solution, easier reuse of tooling from other ecosystems, and the bugs and gaps in the specification that reimplementing something always turns up. The fifth is the one that reads like a governance argument rather than an engineering one: a single implementation is a single point of failure and a central point of control, which is an odd thing for a network built on decentralisation to depend on. That case, more than the amount, is probably why this was the only proposal of the five nobody seriously argued with.

## Two budgets fail, and not on their contents

[Cardano Treasury DeFi Liquidity Budget](/ga/4840e305563327358cf70dae5015b2df8f8c35cef03f74521d4f117ac17bc38400/) closed at 4.05% of counted DRep power on 10 yes ballots to 135 no, the weakest result the series has recorded, with no committee member in favour and none of the pools that voted supporting it either.

[2025 Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 7.5M ₳ for community builders](/ga/defbf15b06092718adf4befeab982e03d2966b9caeef93c19e470549ef75ea4900/) closed at 34.49%, and it is the more interesting failure, because it was never really a funding request. It is an objection to how the main budget was assembled. Its argument is that the off chain poll applied its majority threshold to the ada that happened to vote rather than to all the registered voting stake, which lets a proposal through on a low turnout, and that on the corrected bar of ₳2.69B a different set of proposals qualifies. So it bundled the community proposals that clear that bar and left out the ones from the founding entities, partly because a proposer going on chain alone needs a ₳100k deposit that a small builder may not have.

All seven committee members found it unconstitutional, and the published reason is narrow and procedural: the constitution requires a budget to name one or more administrators and to specify how the use of withdrawn funds will be overseen, and this action did neither. Nothing in that finding is about the projects it listed or the argument it made. The aggregate budget it was objecting to does name an administrator, which is part of why one was judged constitutional and the other was not.

This is where the difference between kinds of information action matters. A signal about a hard fork name records an opinion and nothing else. A budget or a ceiling is an information action too, so the ledger never ratifies it, but the constitution makes both of them preconditions for spending money and gives the committee something concrete to rule on. Calling them non binding describes the ledger, not the process.

## Two ceilings fall short, and the ₳350M one stands by default

[2025 Cardano NCL](/ga/7d9fc9fe4cee64fb34e57783378ac869a85c78d6fbcd4078ed131ab6fa3c7db600/), which asked for ₳200M over the period already covered by the ₳350M ceiling, closed at the start of epoch 561 with 43.88% of counted DRep power, on 213 yes ballots to 85 no and ₳2.35B of yes power against ₳2.17B. [Set a 300 million ADA Net Change Limit for Epochs 563 to 635](/ga/637e3c256da9c1350847ca3211e8c44e3660a4471e8b6f68a8c537746b4aeb7300/) closed at the same boundary as the budgets with 37.26%, on 109 yes to 87 no. Six committee members voted yes on each of them and one abstained: both were found constitutional, and neither was adopted.

The effect is that the ₳350M ceiling endorsed in epoch 554, running to the end of epoch 604, remained the only one in force. Two attempts to lower it and one to set a different period on top of it had now been put and not taken.

## What has to happen before anyone is paid

[Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 275M ada Administered by Intersect](/ga/e14de8d9dc4f4ddf3fe9250a8a926e20f10e99b86bd0610b77d7a054981591ee00/) was one epoch from its deadline and stood at 65.4% of counted DRep power as of the close of epoch 563. Endorsing it still moves no money: each of the 39 proposals inside it needs a treasury withdrawal action of its own, and each of those has to fit under a ceiling that is in force at the time. The [review of epochs 564 to 569](/governance-review/epochs-564-569/) has the result and the first withdrawal request.

Across the three epochs DReps cast 374 ballots, committee members 32 and pools 43, 449 in all with superseded votes included, and 151 distinct DReps cast a final ballot. The power delegated to DReps went from ₳5.32B at epoch 561 to ₳5.37B at epoch 563, and the treasury reading rose ₳8.5M to ₳1.78B with nothing paid out of it.
