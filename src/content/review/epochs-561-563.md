## A second node is the one thing DReps agreed on

[Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget: Amaru Node Development 2025](/ga/bd488931f792651fefa9c6fda185a2c6cec83245b51d994e33090ce36e29cc2600/) closed at the start of epoch 563 with 80.37% of DRep power behind it, on 232 yes ballots to 14 no and 10 abstain, ₳4.32B of power for it against ₳28.7M. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. What it asks for is ₳1.5M towards Amaru, a second program capable of producing Cardano blocks, written mainly in Rust and built under a not for profit association of several Cardano companies rather than by one of them. Cardano had run on a single implementation since launch, and the proposal gives five reasons to change that: lower resource use from different design trade offs, monitoring and tracing that follow industry standards rather than a bespoke solution, easier reuse of tooling from other ecosystems, and the bugs and gaps in the specification that reimplementing something always turns up. The fifth is the one that reads like a governance argument rather than an engineering one: a single implementation is a single point of failure and a central point of control. The budget also does what the constitution asks of one: it names an administrator, the project's own maintainer committee, with a smart contract to hold the funds, and that detail decides the next case.

The largest DReps agreed with the engineering argument. [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) wrote that the existing node "leaves developers with little flexibility" and that it has confidence in PRAGMA, the association behind the project. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) wrote that the budget corresponds directly to the roadmap the DReps had approved and that the project "has the qualities of a public good". The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) put it in one line, that node diversity is important. Pi Lanningham abstained over a conflict of interest, as a scope owner whose company would be one of the development teams, while writing that a reimplementation forces every design decision to be "opened and re-examined". The one committee abstention, from the Cardano Japan Council, touched the administrator: a maintainer committee made up "exclusively of core project members" raises concerns about independence and outside oversight.

## Two budgets fail, and not on their contents

[Cardano Treasury DeFi Liquidity Budget](/ga/4840e305563327358cf70dae5015b2df8f8c35cef03f74521d4f117ac17bc38400/) closed at 4.05% of DRep power on 10 yes ballots to 135 no, the lowest support of the five proposals closing in this window, with no committee member in favour and none of the pools that voted supporting it either. The number says less than it seems. The document the action pointed to did not match the hash recorded on chain, and the proposer had said it would resubmit. YUTA voted no "to prevent the proposal from being adopted twice", [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) because the action "is invalid and will reportedly be resubmitted", and the Intersect Constitutional Council found it unconstitutional for the mismatch alone. The resubmitted version is the fund the [review of epochs 588 to 590](/governance-review/epochs-588-590/) covers.

[2025 Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 7.5M ₳ for community builders](/ga/defbf15b06092718adf4befeab982e03d2966b9caeef93c19e470549ef75ea4900/) closed at 34.49%, and it is the more interesting failure, because it combined a funding request for seven community projects with a challenge to how the main budget was assembled. Its argument is that the off chain poll applied its majority threshold to the ada that happened to vote rather than to all the registered voting stake, which lets a proposal through on a low turnout, and that on the bar its authors considered correct, ₳2.69B, a different set of proposals qualifies. So it bundled the community proposals that clear that bar and left out the ones from the founding entities, partly because a proposer going on chain alone needs a ₳100k deposit that a small builder may not have.

All seven committee members found it unconstitutional, and the Eastern Cardano Council's published reason is narrow and procedural: the constitution requires a budget to designate responsible administrators and to specify how the use of withdrawn funds will be overseen, and this action did neither. Nothing in that finding is about the projects it listed or the argument it made. The DReps quoted here did not dispute the projects either. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) wrote that "we support every proposal listed" but that all of them were already in the larger budget process, so approving them again "risks double-funding". EMURGO had voted for most of them in that process and voted no here for the same overlap. Dave supported several of the projects, among them Midgard, the Dolos node and Starstream, and voted no because each should be voted on individually and because at least one of them had said publicly it did not agree to the administration proposed. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) abstained because no process existed for choosing which version of a proposal applies when it sits in both bundles. CardanoYoda voted yes, having supported six of the seven projects and counting three of them as answers to scalability, "the most pressing need". The aggregate budget it was objecting to does name an administrator, and so does the node budget, which is part of why those two were judged constitutional and this one was not.

This is where the difference between kinds of information action matters. A signal about a hard fork name records an opinion and nothing else. A budget or a ceiling is an information action too, so the ledger never ratifies it, but the constitution makes both of them preconditions for spending money and gives the committee something concrete to rule on. Calling them non binding describes the ledger, not the process.

## Two ceilings fall short, and the ₳350M one stands by default

[2025 Cardano NCL](/ga/7d9fc9fe4cee64fb34e57783378ac869a85c78d6fbcd4078ed131ab6fa3c7db600/), which asked for ₳200M over the period already covered by the ₳350M ceiling, closed at the start of epoch 561 with 43.88% of DRep power, on 213 yes ballots to 85 no and ₳2.35B of yes power against ₳2.17B. [Set a 300 million ADA Net Change Limit for Epochs 563 to 635](/ga/637e3c256da9c1350847ca3211e8c44e3660a4471e8b6f68a8c537746b4aeb7300/) closed at the same boundary as the budgets with 37.26%, on 109 yes to 87 no. It asked for ₳300M over a period of its own starting at epoch 563, on the reasoning that the limits so far only ran to the end of 2025 while the budgets being proposed were built on twelve month timelines, and its text said it would replace any previous ceiling if agreed. Six committee members voted yes on each of them and one abstained: both were found constitutional, and neither was adopted.

Yoroi and EMURGO, two of the three largest DReps, opposed both, and for a reason that had nothing to do with the figures. Yoroi wrote that it had voted for the ₳350M limit and "will continue to align with that voting stance". EMURGO wrote on both that "repeatedly adjusting the cap could create delays" and that the limit already approved is a maximum, "not a fixed target that must be fully used". The Eternl DRep Committee split them: no to ₳200M because it would delay funding for every project except those of the largest proposer, yes to ₳300M because it "should cover most proposal costs" into the next year. YUTA voted yes on both, finding ₳350M too much for half a year and ₳200M too little for budgets that run twelve months. [SASA_nagamaru_ながまる](/dreps/drep1ytuddrgqca0xd4aams6pl9zndpcmxg0rs7z3mggjelm6kscp4arg5/) voted no on the lower one because the largest requests already came to 193,000,000 ada, and yes on the other as "the more cautious end" of a realistic range. CardanoYoda voted yes on ₳300M as the endorsed budget of about 281,000,000 ada plus a margin, and [BTBF_XSP](/dreps/drep1y2vyrqh0fa4qh4tmy7322va6ryk78uh6szppezaft0watyqp5hdwx/) no, because a period spanning two calendar years "creates an awkward timeframe". SIPO voted no and yes in the same pattern, on the argument that proposals running into the next year would otherwise have no limit to draw under.

The effect is that the ₳350M ceiling endorsed in epoch 554, running to the end of epoch 604, remained the only one in force. Two attempts to lower it and one to set a different period on top of it had now been put and not taken.

## What has to happen before anyone is paid

[Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 275M ada Administered by Intersect](/ga/e14de8d9dc4f4ddf3fe9250a8a926e20f10e99b86bd0610b77d7a054981591ee00/) stood at 65.4% of DRep power as of the close of epoch 563, with voting ending at the start of epoch 564. Endorsing it still moves no money: each of the 39 proposals inside it needs a treasury withdrawal action of its own, and each of those has to fit under a ceiling that is in force at the time. The [review of epochs 564 to 569](/governance-review/epochs-564-569/) has the result, the reasons of the DReps on both sides, and the first withdrawal request.

Across the three epochs DReps cast 374 ballots, committee members 32 and pools 43, 449 in all with superseded votes included, and 151 distinct DReps cast a final ballot. The power delegated to DReps went from ₳5.32B at epoch 561 to ₳5.37B at epoch 563, and the treasury reading rose ₳8.5M to ₳1.78B with nothing paid out of it.
