## One period ends, another begins, and nothing carries over

The spending period that began at epoch 532 closed at the end of epoch 612, eight epochs later than originally planned, an extension the [review of epochs 603 to 605](/governance-review/epochs-603-605/) explains. It finished with ₳347.0M of its ₳350M ceiling drawn across 40 paid withdrawals, 99.1% of what it allowed, leaving ₳2.97M unused.

That remainder is not a credit. A ceiling caps what may leave the treasury inside its own period, and when the period ends the unused part ends with it. [Net Change Limit (Epoch 613 to Epoch 713)](/ga/dc4c679c8cf1cec49817d4d2c1c96cd802ec8a047a11dc0b0bb125b5af0a76cd00/), endorsed at the start of epoch 612 with 61.33% of counted DRep power, opens a fresh ₳350M for the hundred epochs from 613 to 713, and the new period started at zero.

Its document says how the figure was chosen. It takes the treasury's inflows of 2025, ₳306.9M across epochs 532 to 604, and sets the ceiling at about 115% of them. The part above the inflows has a named purpose: the ₳50M stablecoin liquidity budget the DReps had approved in 2025, which nobody had yet drawn on when the ceiling was submitted on behalf of the budget committee at Intersect, and which the committee chose to make room for. The first draw on that budget is in this window too, further down, and it did not get through.

[Net Change Limit of 300 Million ADA for Epochs 613 to 713](/ga/73a4eb2148781c37ef37c90a33a1d3d00511a8eefe9cdfaa1ea593b090f23f9600/), proposing a lower ceiling for exactly the same epochs, was still open at the close with a deadline at the start of epoch 618. Its document starts from the same ₳306.9M of inflows and sets the limit at that figure rather than above it, so the ₳50M between the two proposals is, in substance, whether the period holds room for the liquidity budget. Two endorsed ceilings for one period would be a problem worth writing about, and the [review of epochs 615 to 620](/governance-review/epochs-615-620/) has the result.

## More memory for smart contracts

[Increase Transaction and Block Memory Units (Part 1 of 2)](/ga/c21b00f90f18fce4003edf42b0b0d455126e01c946e80cc5341a9f9750caf79500/) was ratified at the start of epoch 612 and took effect at epoch 613, with 86.45% of counted DRep power and 57.16% of counted pool stake behind it, both above their bars, and six committee members in favour.

Every Cardano transaction carries a budget for how much work a smart contract inside it may do, measured in memory units, and there is a second budget for a whole block. Reach either ceiling and the transaction cannot be built, which is a hard limit on how complex a single on chain operation can be. This change raises the per transaction limit from 14,000,000 units to 16,500,000 and the per block limit from 62,000,000 to 72,000,000, and touches nothing else. Its abstract calls that a 25% step, its own figures work out to 17.9% and 16.1%, and the text explains the sizes as the most the guardrails recommend while keeping four maximally sized transactions in one block. It also explains why it is called part one of two: a guardrail requires a change of this kind to be split across two linked actions.

## Dropped, not expired

[Reduce minimum Constitutional Committee size (committeeMinSize) from 7 to 5](/ga/dfdac5921ab657241fce58583d61bef59a369e01d2ba78191d6df6632a07fdfd00/) left the governance queue at epoch 614, and its outcome in the record is neither ratified nor expired but dropped. That is a third possibility this series has not met before, and it is worth understanding, because it means a proposal can lose without anybody voting it down.

A parameter change has to name the parameter change that came before it. That pointer is how the ledger keeps a chain of parameter changes in order, and it has to name the most recently enacted one. This proposal, filed in epoch 612, named the Plutus cost model change an [earlier edition](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/) covers, which was the most recent enacted parameter change at the time it was written. When the memory increase was enacted at the start of epoch 613, that was no longer true. The pointer was stale, the proposal could no longer be applied to the chain it claimed to extend, and the ledger removed it.

Its 19.19% of counted DRep power was never going to carry it past a 75% bar, so the outcome is not in doubt. The mechanism is the point: two competing parameter changes are not two candidates in one race, and the first one enacted can knock the other out procedurally. The attempt to lower the committee minimum from seven to five was itself a response to the resignation of the [review of epochs 597 to 599](/governance-review/epochs-597-599/), which had shown what a committee at exactly its minimum does when someone leaves.

## A name for the next upgrade

[Name Protocol Version 11 hard fork - van Rossem](/ga/8845bfc37bb2f69e8f200fe28148b3dea3c4399b0c49ee0ed2bb4e349cab9eb700/) closed at the start of epoch 613 with 83.62% of counted DRep power behind it and 86 pool ballots in favour, none against.

It asks for the next hard fork to carry the name of Max van Rossem, a community contributor who had recently died. Its text sets out why: he was a member and co lead of the working group that ran the first election of a fully elected constitutional committee, he represented his national community as a delegate at the constitutional convention that produced the constitution, and he was among the driving forces behind one of its articles. Cardano has named upgrades after contributors who died before, and the [edition on the Plomin rename](/governance-review/epochs-523-525/) covers the last time this series reported one.

## Also in the window

[A treasury withdrawal without a title in the record](/ga/4b10e5793208cb8f228756e02113227c91602248eac4d992681a0ee760b6c4e200/), the ₳500,000 first payment against the stablecoin liquidity budget the new ceiling was sized to hold, expired at the start of epoch 614 after all six voting committee members found it unconstitutional, for the two omissions the [review of epochs 606 to 608](/governance-review/epochs-606-608/) sets out. [Amaru Treasury Withdrawal 2026](/ga/2f2fc2539dde550d3c83bea67c11b03861407976bbe8d07c5b0e124aa8a12baa00/) was filed in epoch 614, a ₳10.14M request for a second year of work on the alternative node, with a deadline at the start of epoch 621. Its document says the request had been held back because the limit then in force had no room for it, which is why it arrived one epoch after the new period opened. The sum covers a year priced at fifty cents per ada, January and February 2026 included after the fact, with a relay capable node due in the first quarter and a block producing node by the middle of the year, and the money is to be administered directly by the project's maintainer committee through a smart contract that can return unspent contingency to the treasury.

The window recorded 800 ballots, 441 from DReps, 346 from pools and 13 from committee members, with final ballots from 179 distinct DReps and 295 pools. Pools turned out for the memory change, which needs their stake as well as the DReps. The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.84B at epoch 614 across 866 DReps, the ten largest holding 48.06% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳8.6M to ₳1.64B, with the new ceiling untouched.
