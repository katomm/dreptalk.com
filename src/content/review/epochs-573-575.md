## Three payments, and the difference between paid and approved

At the start of epoch 575 the treasury paid ₳168.4M across three withdrawals. ₳96.8M of it went on [the core engineering payment](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e01/), the largest single payment since on-chain governance began, for a list of work its document spells out: a new consensus protocol, a re-architected node, a state channel layer and a light client protocol, tooling that checks smart contracts before they run, and maintenance, support and audits of the software already in use. ₳69.5M went on [the community funding payment](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e17/), the programme through which the community hands out smaller grants of its own. Its document puts ₳61M of that into three funding rounds and the rest into the interfaces and infrastructure the programme runs on. ₳2.2M went on [the rollup payment](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e00/), for work on settling batches of transactions on the main chain rather than each one individually.

All three were ratified at the start of epoch 574 with more than 72% of DRep power behind them and paid one epoch later. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no.

## ₳96.8M for core engineering, and the Cardano Foundation abstains

Almost nobody voted against ₳96.8M for core engineering: ₳3.81B of delegated power voted yes and ₳0.02B no. What the rationales record is a reservation on the yes side and an abstention from one of the largest DReps. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) called it "a strategic investment" in protocol upgrades and scaling layers, reviewed by the technical steering committee, and [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) "a structural and long-term investment". The [Cardano Foundation](/dreps/drep1ydpfkyjxzeqvalf6fgvj7lznrk8kcmfnvy9hyl6gr6ez6wgsjaelx/) abstained, in one document that sets out its ballot on all 39 requests: on this one it was "not confident that the current version offers sufficient transparency or accountability", because scope, cost effectiveness and safeguards had not been adjusted after community feedback. The same document abstains on the ₳26.8M research request as "materially too high", votes yes on the ₳69.5M community funding payment "to ensure continuity for the main source of grant funding" in a transition year, and turns an earlier abstention on the ₳15.8M request of the administering organisation into a yes after that budget was revised.

The no votes were few and small. Maureen wrote that ₳96.8M reads like "a university research department's wishlist" and that the proposal should "pick three things and do them excellently". A DRep without a published name found the technical steering committee's alternative much more mature and assigning everything to one company unsustainable. Cardanians abstained, calling the work non negotiable but its success dependent on transparent delivery. On the community funding payment, InputEndorsers abstained because it had been a paid reviewer of the programme for years and did not want to be "a tipping vote", No stress voted no in favour of "less than 20 million per round and less than 3 rounds per year", and Medusa DRep voted no because it prefers "larger number of smaller withdrawals" and found no account of how the programme's existing funds are used. On the rollup payment Cardano Cypherpunks voted no for lack of "a business justification".

27 more requests were ratified at the start of epoch 575, ₳78.1M between them, and none of that had been paid by the close. A withdrawal is ratified at one boundary and paid at the next, so approved money still sits in the treasury and still counts as unspent against the ceiling. The ₳180.1M the ceiling still allowed at epoch 575 therefore overstates what was actually free: ₳78.1M of it was already promised. The two figures in the treasury readings are worth keeping apart as well. ₳168.4M went out, but the balance fell ₳160.1M between the epoch 573 and epoch 575 readings, because the treasury kept taking its share of rewards over the same epochs.

Nine of the original 39 requests were still being voted on at the close, all nine of them from the transaction that carried 37 of the batch, with the boundary to epoch 577 as their deadline. The [review of epochs 576 to 578](/governance-review/epochs-576-578/) has the rest.

## The governance software budget passes, on price objections

[Cardano GovTool Budget - 12 months full active maintenance and development](/ga/9d213a57684d7ddf6f3350c80d042639ecbed5ccccc4a05bf54959a086593e7b00/), the budget for the governance software used to register as a DRep, delegate, submit proposals and vote, closed at the start of epoch 574 with 57.15% of DRep power behind it, on 175 yes ballots, 53 no and 11 abstain, and all seven committee members voting yes. As a budget it still needs a withdrawal action before any of it is paid, and its document says so itself: the ₳1.15M it asks for, twelve months of maintenance and development of the tool, would come through a separate withdrawal action once the info action had shown support.

The yes votes were about what the tool is. The objections were about cost, reliability and delivery accountability. Yoroi called it "critical, open-source governance infrastructure" and noted the team's response to recent bugs, while conceding that delivery structure and operational clarity still need work. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) voted yes because barriers to taking part "are still very big" and most wallets give governance functions "low priority", so only three wallets meet its criteria for finding and delegating to a DRep. The Cardano Foundation called its yes "a pragmatic decision" to keep the primary non commercial tool viable rather than rely on closed source alternatives. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) voted yes after leaning the other way, writing that 1,150,000 ada, about 920,000 dollars for 5.75 full time equivalents, "seemed quite steep" and that 1,000,000 dollars a year "should be enough to support several tools, not just one".

Most of the abstentions and no votes come back to the sum. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) abstained because the amount "is way too high for the proposed work and what has beed delivered so far", offering to help host the tool if no other solution were available. EMURGO abstained "not out of opposition, but out of caution", wanting execution risk and delivery accountability spelled out. [Sebastien Guillemot セバ](/dreps/drep1y2csyxt7u2hl4674pl9cef5lknafaw5nraxvyx033kmd0es3awuv0/) voted no because the interface "has always been confusing and burdensome" and the sum is a lot for a product he thinks delivered a bad experience. Kyle Solomon put keeping the lights on at under 50,000 dollars a year with serverless infrastructure and voted no. Medusa DRep, which runs a cluster of nodes, an indexer and a frontend for "around $100 per month", voted no against the roughly 27,500 dollars a month requested for infrastructure and maintenance, a comparison that sets a hosting bill against the proposal's foundational tier, 330,000 dollars a year for a maintenance team of two full time equivalents as well as hosting. Dave voted no because the tool's "current unreliability impedes effective governance participation", and Dan changed a yes to an abstention after encountering errors several times while trying to vote on the withdrawals through it. The withdrawal that follows this budget arrives in the [review of epochs 582 to 584](/governance-review/epochs-582-584/).

[Tempo for Cardono Governance - Maintenance & Development Budget for 2025](/ga/495bd3cd44ea6bc0b4c2dbb1e4cdc7e214874c2c07490a4e2476a627ef91128000/), for the independent voting application built for phones, reached its last epoch of voting here. It asks for ₳380,000 over six months, ₳200,000 of it to keep running its own node and indexer and ₳180,000 for development. As of the close of epoch 575 it stood at 40.95% of DRep power, with three committee members for it and four against. The [review of epochs 576 to 578](/governance-review/epochs-576-578/) covers that boundary.

## One plan, two requests, and a factor of a million

Two withdrawal actions for the same idea arrived in epoch 573, both from the Snek Foundation and both to put the SNEK token onto large exchanges, with liquidity provision, legal work and an advisory board, on the argument that Cardano native tokens are barely present on the platforms most people actually use. The foundation's own account is that it has already paid for two large exchange listings out of its own funds and wants the community to fund the next ones. Its document asks for 5,000,000 ada.

[Withdraw ₳5M for Cardano's Global Listing Expansion - Powered by Snek](/ga/1d6aea56e6a523b13e03ae30ff24ebf9852543b4f7d415c7e50f55f6baaa4e3d00/) asks the ledger for exactly that. [Cardano Global Listing Expansion - Powered by Snek](/ga/4ff43f1eab5252ef00ba6f1429dc23715e3899834f10c067e8065c8364d5559a00/), filed in the same epoch, carries a payload of ₳5. The record stores what each action asks the ledger for, and in the second case that is five ada against a document asking for five million, a million fold gap that looks like an amount entered in the wrong unit, though the record does not establish the cause. Both had the boundary to epoch 580 as their deadline, and the [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/) has what the DReps and the committee made of them.

## Two changes to the rules join the queue

The window also brought the two actions that will decide who judges constitutionality and what the constitution says. [Replace Interim Constitutional Committee](/ga/47a0e7a4f9383b1afc2192b23b41824d65ac978d7741aca61fc1fa16833d111100/) was filed in epoch 573. Its document says the term of the committee appointed for the trial phase ends with epoch 580, and it asks the DReps and the pools to seat the seven members chosen in an election held off chain, three of them for 146 epochs and four for 73, so that the seats never all come up at once. Three of the seven already sit on the interim committee. The action's own voting deadline is the start of epoch 580, and the interim committee's term ends with that epoch.

[CARDANO BLOCKCHAIN ECOSYSTEM CONSTITUTION v2.0](/ga/207b226e110e13bb18b119fcd313520e0fcd060b2bc9fb9a5e5bc6e94ab10f3b00/) followed in epoch 574. Its document lists wording, definitions and the removal of provisions never used, and one change it singles out as substantive: to drop the requirement that a treasury withdrawal be preceded by a budget info action. That is the rule this window runs on: every one of the 39 requests says in its document that it gives effect to the budget agreed in epoch 564, and the two governance tools above are budgets waiting for a withdrawal action to follow. Its vote runs to the boundary to epoch 581. The [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/) has both results.

## The workload, in ballots

The window recorded 4,139 ballots, 3,785 from DReps, 271 from committee members and 83 from pools. The committee figure is the new one: 5 ballots in the whole previous window, 271 here, because every request needs a constitutionality finding of its own and the batch arrived at once. On the DRep side the 3,785 ballots came from 226 distinct voters, out of 845 holding delegated power at epoch 575.

The power delegated to DReps rose ₳91.2M across the window to ₳5.39B, held by 845 DReps, the most up to then, with the ten largest holding 47.26% of it. The count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 336 to 343.
