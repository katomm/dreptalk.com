## ₳50M that would not be spent

[Stablecoin DeFi Liquidity Budget](/ga/e5643c33f608642e329228a968770e5b19ef5f48ff1f698712e2ce864a49e3f000/), filed in epoch 582, asks for 50,000,000 ada and proposes to do something different with it from every budget the series has covered so far. The money would not be paid to anyone for work. It would go into a fund of ada and fiat backed stablecoins, and 99% of it would be placed with decentralised exchanges and lending protocols as liquidity, with the treasury holding a position rather than making a grant. Its text puts revenue for the treasury as a secondary goal, names a nine person interim committee of publicly identified individuals to administer the fund from a smart contract, and describes a new treasury organisation for oversight. The budget runs for twelve months. Of that, up to ₳500,000 would pay for the legal framework and the contract, the remaining ₳49.5M would go to the protocols, and each month 15% of what the deployed funds earn would be converted to ada and sent back to the treasury, the rest staying in the protocols. The oversight body could remove the committee or shut the fund down and return the money. Its deadline was the start of epoch 589.

## A strategy vote its own authors did not file

[Defining the Cardano 2030 Vision & Strategy](/ga/996edfe370b8e51be73541e75499a818461305e37c7fa0c1b193f2c587167cc700/) arrived in epoch 583 and asks DReps to endorse Cardano's direction to 2030. The proposal is careful about who is asking: the product committee wrote the content, but the action was submitted by a community member acting on their own, to move feedback that had been gathered off chain into a vote on chain. That distinction matters for a signal, because a yes endorses the text rather than the body behind it. The document spells out what each ballot means: a yes supports the strategy, a no does not, and an abstention is neither, and it asks DReps to attach a rationale to their vote. Its deadline was the start of epoch 590.

## The software governance runs on asks to be paid

[Withdraw ₳1,150,000 for GovTool 12 months active maintenance and development](/ga/d2db60c5307cb517c735e2d0138d2b6f10fc5b221d610fa187719bdc82af9a0300/), filed in epoch 584, is the withdrawal that follows the budget endorsed in epoch 574 in the [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/). It covers a year of work on the open source applications most DReps use to register, delegate, submit proposals and vote, which is to say the tools the votes in this article were themselves cast through. Its document names that budget by id as the one it draws on, and says the work is done by a consortium of four builders with other open source contributors, that Intersect would administer the money through a smart contract, and that anything unused goes back to the treasury. The sum is itemised: hosting and infrastructure, development of each part of the tools priced in full time equivalents, ₳150,000 set aside for contributors paid by their contributions, and ₳100,000 for other open source governance tools that build on the same code. Its deadline was the start of epoch 591.

## Nobody had to decide anything yet

None of the three was decided here, and neither were the two proposals carried over from the [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/), the regional plan and the loan version of the token listing plan. Five open proposals, no decisions, nothing paid: the ₳78.0M left under the ceiling at epoch 581 was still ₳78.0M at epoch 584.

Most of the largest DReps had not voted on the new proposals at the close, and it would be a mistake to read anything into that. The three were one to three epochs old, and a DRep has until the deadline. What it does mean is that a large share of the power that will decide them had not yet moved, so early readings on any of the three were worth very little.

## The numbers

The window recorded 319 ballots, 301 from DReps, 8 from pools and 10 from committee members, with final ballots from 132 distinct DReps. At epoch 584, 858 DReps held ₳5.61B of delegated power, the most DReps up to then, and 355 of them had cast a ballot in the previous twelve epochs, down from 382 at epoch 581 as the busy payout epochs dropped out of that window. The ten largest held 49.17% of the power. The treasury reading rose ₳8.2M to ₳1.59B.
