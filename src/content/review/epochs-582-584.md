## A ₳50M fund to provide liquidity

[Stablecoin DeFi Liquidity Budget](/ga/e5643c33f608642e329228a968770e5b19ef5f48ff1f698712e2ce864a49e3f000/), filed in epoch 582, asks for 50,000,000 ada and proposes to do something different with it from every budget the series has covered so far. Most of the money would be deployed as liquidity, with the treasury keeping a claim on the assets, and a smaller part would cover setting the fund up. The money would go into a fund of ada and fiat backed stablecoins, and 99% of it would be placed with decentralised exchanges and lending protocols. Its text puts revenue for the treasury as a secondary goal, names a nine person interim committee of publicly identified individuals to administer the fund from a smart contract, and describes a new treasury organisation for oversight. The budget runs for twelve months. Of that, up to ₳500,000 would pay for the legal framework and the contract, the remaining ₳49.5M is for the protocols, of which its budget table sets aside 500,000 ada for converting ada and minting stablecoins and 49,000,000 ada for deploying liquidity, and each month 15% of what the deployed funds earn would be converted to ada and sent back to the treasury, the rest staying in the protocols. The oversight body could remove the committee or shut the fund down and return the money. Its deadline was the start of epoch 589.

## A strategy vote its own authors did not file

[Defining the Cardano 2030 Vision & Strategy](/ga/996edfe370b8e51be73541e75499a818461305e37c7fa0c1b193f2c587167cc700/) arrived in epoch 583 and asks DReps to endorse Cardano's direction to 2030. The proposal is careful about who is asking: the product committee wrote the content, but the action was submitted by a community member acting on their own, to move feedback that had been gathered off chain into a vote on chain. That distinction matters for a signal, because a yes endorses the text rather than the body behind it. The document spells out what each ballot means: a yes supports the strategy, a no does not, and an abstention is neither, and it asks DReps to attach a rationale to their vote. Its deadline was the start of epoch 590.

## GovTool asks for ₳1.15M for a year of maintenance

[Withdraw ₳1,150,000 for GovTool 12 months active maintenance and development](/ga/d2db60c5307cb517c735e2d0138d2b6f10fc5b221d610fa187719bdc82af9a0300/), filed in epoch 584, is the withdrawal that follows the budget endorsed in epoch 574 in the [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/). It covers a year of work on the open source governance applications used to register as a DRep, delegate, submit proposals and vote, which is to say the tools many of the votes in this article were themselves cast through. Its document names that budget by id as the one it draws on, and says the work is done by a consortium of four builders with other open source contributors, that Intersect would administer the money through a smart contract, and that anything unused goes back to the treasury. The sum is itemised: hosting and infrastructure, development of each part of the tools priced in full time equivalents, ₳150,000 set aside for contributors paid by their contributions, and ₳100,000 for other open source governance tools that build on the same code. Its deadline was the start of epoch 591.

## The first ballots

None of the three was decided here, and neither were the two proposals carried over from the [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/), the regional plan and the loan version of the token listing plan. Nothing was paid, so the ₳78.0M left under the ceiling at epoch 581 was still ₳78.0M at epoch 584. Most of the largest DReps had not voted on the new proposals at the close, which says nothing about their intentions: the three were one to three epochs old, and a DRep has until the deadline. The ballots that were cast, dated by their block time, already show where the arguments would run. No share is given for any of them, and a DRep may still change a ballot until the deadline.

On the liquidity fund the early yes votes were about scale. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) put stablecoins at a market of 282,000,000,000 dollars, of which Cardano's native dollar backed coins held about 25,000,000 dollars, so that ₳50M "could roughly double that amount" and was "the bare minimum needed". [SASA_nagamaru_ながまる](/dreps/drep1ytuddrgqca0xd4aams6pl9zndpcmxg0rs7z3mggjelm6kscp4arg5/) called thin stablecoin liquidity "one of the biggest challenges currently facing Cardano" and noted the simulated gradual sale of ada meant to limit the price impact. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) voted yes as a user who had "many situations" where it would have transacted on chain with more liquidity, and said it would re-read the no rationales in October and reconsider. Kyle Solomon voted yes because the oversight body of DReps could remove administrators or shut the fund down and return the money to the treasury, which he called "a critical component". The one sizeable no in the window came from a DRep without a published name: demand on Cardano's exchanges is weak, "the absence of native USDT/USDC is the fundamental bottleneck", and deploying 50,000,000 ada now is premature.

On the strategy draft the question was whether the vote should exist. Kyle Solomon abstained as vice chair of the product committee that wrote the text, writing that the committee already planned its own information action "circa end of October" and that DReps were being shown "heavily incomplete work". Hephaestus Stake Pool abstained for the same reason, quoting committee members who said the wording had not been signed off. SASA voted no on the content: a target of 200,000,000 dollars of base layer revenue lacks a calculation method or ada price assumptions, and the strategy "reads more like a comprehensive wishlist" than a prioritised plan. CardanoYoda voted no while liking the document, for reasons "partly procedural and partly substantive". YUTA voted yes, treating it as provisional and preferring it to moving forward with a blank vision.

On the tool withdrawal the early ballots favoured the withdrawal, ₳0.62B of delegated power for it against ₳0.24B in epoch 584, with CryptoCrow the largest no and publishing no reason. SASA wrote that it "simply executes the already-approved ₳1.15M budget", YUTA that governance "is in a very difficult situation" and that the tool and wallet providers need to work together, and TriangleForces, one of the no votes, wrote that it supports a community owned tool as a public good, but this request "fails to demonstrate fiscal discipline", so "tighten the ask, come back". Yoroi and EMURGO, the largest and the third largest DReps, had not voted on any of the three, and neither had the Eternl DRep Committee or the Cardano Foundation. The [review of epochs 588 to 590](/governance-review/epochs-588-590/) and the [review of epochs 591 to 593](/governance-review/epochs-591-593/) have the results.

## The numbers

The window recorded 319 ballots, 301 from DReps, 8 from pools and 10 from committee members, with final ballots from 132 distinct DReps. At epoch 584, 858 DReps held ₳5.61B of delegated power, the most DReps up to then, and 355 of them had cast a ballot in the previous twelve epochs, down from 382 at epoch 581 as the busy payout epochs dropped out of that window. The ten largest held 49.17% of the power. The treasury reading rose ₳8.2M to ₳1.59B.
