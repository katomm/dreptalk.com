## The first ceiling

[2025 Net Change Limit](/ga/9b62b3c632f329016a968ac25211825bb4f84b12461121c7da3aa11df92370f900/) closed at the start of epoch 554 with 73.35% of DRep power behind it, on 201 yes ballots, 123 no and 13 abstain, ₳3.57B of power for it against ₳0.92B. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. It puts a ceiling of ₳350M on what the treasury may pay out from the start of epoch 532 to the end of epoch 604, a range its text calls 72 epochs although it covers 73, and a period that was already more than twenty epochs old when the vote closed.

The constitution forbids a budget request until a ceiling exists, so until this vote nothing could be asked for and nothing could be paid, and the agreed period began before the vote closed. The ₳350M is not money committed to anything: it is the most that may leave the treasury before the end of epoch 604, and at this point none of it had. The figure itself was agreed with the Cardano Budget Committee and sized to what the treasury actually took in over the previous year, ₳336.0M across epochs 459 to 531, and the document names its own bar: more than 50% of the active voting DRep stake, the figure the constitution sets for a spending limit, which 73.35% clears with room to spare. The pools recorded 42 no ballots to 27 yes, which changes nothing, because a pool ballot carries no weight on an information action.

## A maximum, or a target

The yes votes kept returning to one distinction. [SASA_nagamaru_ながまる](/dreps/drep1ytuddrgqca0xd4aams6pl9zndpcmxg0rs7z3mggjelm6kscp4arg5/) wrote that the limit "is not a 'must spend' limit but a 'can't spend more than this' limit", and that the budget Intersect was proposing came in below it. AdaStat voted yes because the sum "does not actually mean all this money will definitely be taken", since DReps decide each withdrawal, and because an imperfect step beats staying stuck. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) found ₳350M "higher than we would have preferred" and voted yes to get the budget process under way, and [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) because a year of regulatory change would bring opportunities worth the flexibility. Two of the largest yes votes were second choices. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/), the largest DRep, preferred the community alternative of ₳300M for the year and ₳250M for the next, because income in the current year is clearly lower than the year before, but voted yes on both so that the worst outcome, no limit at all, could not happen. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) had opposed ₳350M for the same alternative and changed its vote once that alternative failed, since a second failure "would effectively halt all budget-related actions" for the year.

The no votes took the same distinction the other way. HOSKY wrote that a maximum equal to a whole year of income "risks framing it as a target rather than a safeguard". Cerkoryn wrote that a limit this high "risks incentivizing the community to withdraw funds up to the maximum". InputEndorsers did the arithmetic: the treasury's actual income over the previous year was 335,957,093 ada, so the limit sits roughly 14,000,000 ada above it, in a first year when "our ability to audit, prevent waste and abuse are questionable". Duc Tiger voted no because a limit close to annual income "would rapidly deplete the treasury", and Hephaestus Stake Pool while praising the proposal's format, because two competing limits were on the table at once. [hix_coffeepool☕️](/dreps/drep1ygnh2uf4wkc8ldgfxwz7rzuga3m8jtqew9xh5g3n587mg6g3ge0sj/) abstained, unable to back either answer: fewer withdrawals are better for decentralisation, but keeping unnecessary ada in the treasury is also unhealthy for the ecosystem.

Five committee members voted yes, one no and one abstained, for a share of 83.33%. The no is the odd one: the Intersect Constitutional Council found the action unconstitutional over a typing error, because the proposal describes its period as 72 epochs from the start of epoch 532 to the conclusion of epoch 604, which is 73 epochs and runs a week into the following year. The author confirmed the slip, and the council wrote that choosing between the two readings was "outside of the Constitutional Committee power", so the proposal should have been resubmitted. The Cardano Japan Council recorded a debate of its own: three of its members held that a limit calls for a committee vote because it affects the budget, two would have abstained, and none found it unconstitutional.

## Two rival ceilings, three budgets

With a limit in place, five information actions followed within two epochs, and they fall into two groups.

Two proposed different ceilings. [2025 Cardano NCL](/ga/7d9fc9fe4cee64fb34e57783378ac869a85c78d6fbcd4078ed131ab6fa3c7db600/), filed in epoch 554, asks for ₳200M over exactly the period that had just been settled, from epoch 532 to the end of epoch 604, and states that where the two conflict it supersedes the earlier ceiling. Its argument is not that the ₳350M figure is wrong about income but that the first ceiling should be set low: the community had never paid core developers before, the budget and audit processes were still being worked out, and a limit for the following year could be raised once there was experience to go on. [Set a 300 million ADA Net Change Limit for Epochs 563 to 635](/ga/637e3c256da9c1350847ca3211e8c44e3660a4471e8b6f68a8c537746b4aeb7300/) followed in epoch 556 with ₳300M for a period of its own that begins at epoch 563, which overlaps the one running to the end of epoch 604 rather than following it. Its reason is timing: the ceilings on the table only ran to the end of 2025, while the budgets being proposed were built on twelve month timelines that reach into 2026, and its text says it would replace any previous ceiling if agreed. Two ceilings covering the same epochs is a problem the series has to come back to.

The first ballots on the lower ceiling were in before the window closed, and no share is given here because voting ran to the start of epoch 561. Dave voted yes in epoch 554, preferring a limit of 100,000,000 to 150,000,000 ada and taking ₳200M as a sensible step. Martin Lang voted no the same day for the plainest reason available: it had voted yes on the limit that passed the day before. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) voted no in epoch 554 while calling it "one of the toughest governance decisions so far", having itself asked for a lower limit. SASA voted no in epoch 555 because the three largest requests already came to 193,000,000 ada, and YUTA voted yes on both ceilings in epoch 556, finding ₳350M too much for half a year and ₳200M too little for budgets that run twelve months.

Three asked to spend. [Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget: Amaru Node Development 2025](/ga/bd488931f792651fefa9c6fda185a2c6cec83245b51d994e33090ce36e29cc2600/) asks for ₳1.5M for the second half of 2025 to fund a second block producing node, written mainly in Rust under a not for profit association of several Cardano companies. The point of the association is that the project does not depend on a single company for its funding, and the budget names the project's own maintainer committee as administrator, with a smart contract to hold the money. Pi Lanningham, a scope owner on the project, abstained in epoch 556 over the conflict of interest while writing of the need for a second node. [Cardano Treasury DeFi Liquidity Budget](/ga/4840e305563327358cf70dae5015b2df8f8c35cef03f74521d4f117ac17bc38400/) is for putting treasury funds to work as liquidity. [2025 Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 7.5M ₳ for community builders](/ga/defbf15b06092718adf4befeab982e03d2966b9caeef93c19e470549ef75ea4900/) bundles the proposals from community builders that cleared ₳2.69B of support in the off chain poll, the bar its authors read the constitution as requiring, and leaves out the founding entities, on the argument that a builder who cannot put up the ₳100k deposit alone should not be shut out of the on chain vote. The Tempo DRep voted yes on it in epoch 556 because the projects had passed the off chain check with more than 50% of DReps at reasonable cost. All five were still open at the close of epoch 556. The [review of epochs 557 to 560](/governance-review/epochs-557-560/) follows the votes as they run, and the [review of epochs 561 to 563](/governance-review/epochs-561-563/) has all five results.
