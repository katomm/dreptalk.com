## The work is unpaid, and that decides who does it

The constitutional committee has one job and no salary. It rules on whether a governance action is constitutional, and the constitution ties its hands deliberately: it may not vote on whether a proposal is a good idea, only on whether it breaks the rules. Carrying that out means reading every proposal, knowing the text it is measured against, deliberating, writing up a reasoned finding and meeting people in other time zones to do it.

[Constitutional Committee Compensation Epochs 581-653](/ga/d2745225498d1c56c0f01be9971074a49144d625df0e73a86c51689624fbadb000/) asked for ₳1M to pay for that across the current term. Its argument is about access rather than reward. Stake pool operators are paid by the protocol and DReps may be paid by the ada holders who delegate to them, but a committee member has no such mechanism. Without compensation from a neutral source, the proposal argues, the seats go either to people wealthy enough to subsidise their own service or to people whose employer subsidises it, and it uses the phrase ivory tower for where that ends up. The proposal came out of discussion among the seven serving members, five of whom sought compensation while two asked for nothing. Each of the five could draw up to ₳200,000, released once every six epochs in proportion to the time served, through a separate contract per member, and the money would only move on a withdrawal action of its own that all five had to approve before it was submitted.

It closed at the start of epoch 596 with 21.18% of counted DRep power behind it, on 48 yes ballots, 105 no and 27 abstain.

## More weight abstained than voted

The percentage is not the interesting part of the result. ₳1.69B of delegated power abstained, more than the weight on either side, in the second window running where the largest single block on a proposal was the abstaining one.

An abstention is a position, and it is not a boycott. Nobody published a reason, which leaves the obvious question open rather than answered: a proposal built by the committee for the committee puts DReps in the position of voting on the pay of the body that rules on their proposals, and abstaining is one way to decline that. Whether that is what happened, or whether DReps objected to the amount, the timing or the principle, the record does not say.

There is a practical point the proposal leaves for later too. A budget endorsement is not money: paying the committee would still need a withdrawal action of its own, with the 67% bar that comes with it, and at 21.18% this budget never got that far.

## Three deadlines at the same boundary

Three proposals reached their last epoch of voting here, all closing at the start of epoch 597. [Loan ₳5,000,000 to Expand Cardano's Global Listings](/ga/03f671791fd97011f30e4d6b76c9a91f4f6bcfb60ee37e5399b9545bb3f2757a00/) asks the community for ₳5M as a loan rather than a grant, to pay for listings of a community token on centralised exchanges, with the sum to be repaid with interest within five years and Intersect administering the money. It is the withdrawal that follows the loan budget of the [review of epochs 585 to 587](/governance-review/epochs-585-587/). [Reimburse Ikigai Info Governance Action Deposit.](/ga/3285b7fd0da16d21e0b8f8910c37f77e17a57cfff8f513df4baf69295480108800/) asks the community for ₳103,000 to make good the ₳100,000 deposit lost on one of the earliest proposals this series covered, the note of thanks the [first edition](/governance-review/epochs-508-513/) covers, which a bug in the node let through with a stake key that could never reclaim it, plus ₳3,000 for the rewards forgone since. [Securing  Generic Top-Level Domains for the Cardano Ecosystem](/ga/a36eafaea085b77f97cceacf07fe9450f8c6b47fec3af94da8f7d158a1fc972200/) asks for support for an application, paid for by the applicant itself and not by the treasury, to register .ada and .cardano as internet domain endings, the part after the last dot in an address. As of that close they stood at 75.89%, 68.16% and 73.83% of counted DRep power. The [review of epochs 597 to 599](/governance-review/epochs-597-599/) has all three results.

## The numbers

The window recorded 296 ballots, 241 from DReps, 37 from pools and 18 from committee members, with final ballots from 82 distinct DReps, the fewest of any window since the constitution vote. The count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs fell to 290 at epoch 596, the lowest since epoch 539, as the payout epochs left the rolling window behind. The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.65B at epoch 596 across 853 DReps, the ten largest holding 49.61% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳8.1M to ₳1.64B, with nothing paid out of it since epoch 578.
