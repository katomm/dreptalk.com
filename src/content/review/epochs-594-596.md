## The case for paying the committee

The constitutional committee has one job and no salary. It rules on whether a governance action is constitutional, and the constitution ties its hands deliberately: it may not vote on whether a proposal is a good idea, only on whether it breaks the rules. Carrying that out means reading every proposal, knowing the text it is measured against, deliberating, writing up a reasoned finding and meeting people in other time zones to do it.

[Constitutional Committee Compensation Epochs 581-653](/ga/d2745225498d1c56c0f01be9971074a49144d625df0e73a86c51689624fbadb000/) asked for ₳1M to pay for that across the current term. Its argument is about access rather than reward. Stake pool operators are paid by the protocol and DReps may be paid by the ada holders who delegate to them, but a committee member has no such mechanism. Without compensation from a neutral source, the proposal argues, the seats go either to people wealthy enough to subsidise their own service or to people whose employer subsidises it, and it uses the phrase ivory tower for where that ends up. The proposal came out of discussion among the seven serving members, five of whom sought compensation while two asked for nothing. Each of the five could draw up to ₳200,000, released once every six epochs in proportion to the time served, through a separate contract per member, and the money would only move on a withdrawal action of its own that all five had to approve before it was submitted.

It closed at the start of epoch 596 with 21.2% of DRep power behind it, on 48 yes ballots, 105 no and 27 abstain. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no.

## Abstaining power outweighed either side

The percentage is not the interesting part of the result. ₳1.69B of delegated power abstained, more than the weight on either side, in the second window running where the largest single block on a proposal was the abstaining one.

The two largest abstainers said the same thing: yes to the principle, not to this figure. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) wrote that it "strongly supports" committee members being compensated as the constitution provides, but that the requested ₳1M "lacks a clear explanation of how the total was derived and what underlying assumptions it represents". [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) wrote that a session it had organised with Intersect at the summit in Berlin showed "a gap in how the proposer and the DReps interpret the clarity" of the figure, and that a common forum was needed before moving forward. SASA_nagamaru_ながまる abstained for a different reason, "a direct interest in this proposal". On the committee itself, Tingvard abstained over "conflict of interest, perceived or otherwise", and three members voted yes.

The no votes wanted the order reversed. The [Cardano Foundation](/dreps/drep1ydpfkyjxzeqvalf6fgvj7lznrk8kcmfnvy9hyl6gr6ez6wgsjaelx/) supported the principle and voted no because the proposal "lacks an explanation of how the requested amount was calculated" and an accountability framework, writing that a public framework defining the committee's duties, minimum participation standards and "conditions for non-payment should be established before a budget for compensation is approved". [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) wrote that the proposal "was not preceded by any debate with DReps and the community" and that it "makes no sense to introduce compensation for one group of governance bodies without knowing how we will compensate DReps". [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) voted yes conditionally, "the issue is the amount", and asked the proposers to set out effort by type of governance action before the withdrawal.

There is a practical point the proposal leaves for later too. A budget endorsement is not money: paying the committee would still need a withdrawal action of its own, with the 67% bar that comes with it, and at 21.2% this budget never got that far. For the committee, the window ended as it began: unpaid.

## Three deadlines at the same boundary

Three proposals reached their last epoch of voting here, all closing at the start of epoch 597, and the [review of epochs 597 to 599](/governance-review/epochs-597-599/) has all three results and what the voters made of them.

[Loan ₳5,000,000 to Expand Cardano's Global Listings](/ga/03f671791fd97011f30e4d6b76c9a91f4f6bcfb60ee37e5399b9545bb3f2757a00/) asks the community for ₳5M as a loan rather than a grant, to pay for listings of the community token SNEK on centralised exchanges, with the sum to be repaid with interest within five years and Intersect administering the money. It is the withdrawal that follows the loan budget of the [review of epochs 585 to 587](/governance-review/epochs-585-587/). As of that close it stood at 75.9% of DRep power.

[Reimburse Ikigai Info Governance Action Deposit.](/ga/3285b7fd0da16d21e0b8f8910c37f77e17a57cfff8f513df4baf69295480108800/) asks the community for ₳103,000 to make good the ₳100,000 deposit lost on one of the earliest proposals this series covered, the note of thanks the [first edition](/governance-review/epochs-508-513/) covers, which a bug in the node let through with a stake key that could never reclaim it, plus ₳3,000 for the rewards forgone since. As of that close it stood at 68.2%.

[Securing  Generic Top-Level Domains for the Cardano Ecosystem](/ga/a36eafaea085b77f97cceacf07fe9450f8c6b47fec3af94da8f7d158a1fc972200/) asks for support for an application by the Cardano Foundation, paid for by the Cardano Foundation itself and not by the treasury, to register .ada and .cardano as internet domain endings, the part after the last dot in an address. As of that close it stood at 73.8%.

## The numbers

The window recorded 296 ballots, 241 from DReps, 37 from pools and 18 from committee members, with final ballots from 82 distinct DReps, the fewest of any window since the constitution vote. The count with a ballot in the last 12 epochs fell to 290 at epoch 596, the lowest since epoch 539, as the payout epochs left the rolling measure behind. The power delegated to DReps read ₳5.65B at epoch 596 across 853 DReps, the ten largest holding 49.6% of it. The treasury reading rose ₳8.1M to ₳1.64B, with nothing paid out of it since epoch 578.
