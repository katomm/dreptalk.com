## What flows in stays the same

Each epoch Cardano mints rewards out of its reserves, adds the transaction fees, and takes a fifth off the top for the treasury before the rest is paid to stakers. [Decrease Treasury Tax from 20% to 10%](/ga/941502b0aa104c850d197923259444d2b57cab7af18b63143775465aaacc84f500/) asked for that fifth to become a tenth, arguing that the rate had been set at launch and never revisited. It expired at the start of epoch 546 with 23.22% of counted DRep power behind it, well short of the 67% a change to an economic parameter needs, on 140 yes ballots, 223 no and 11 abstain, ₳0.91B of power for it against ₳2.02B. DReps were against it in every epoch of its run.

The committee was against it too, five members to two, a share of 28.57%. What their published reasons say is worth reading carefully, because they are not about whether a lower tax is a good idea. The committee rules only on whether an action is constitutional, and on the number itself the answer was yes: the constitution permits a treasury share anywhere between 10% and 30%, so a tenth is inside the range. The objection was to what the proposal did not contain. A parameter change has to come with a plan for reverting it, and it has to address the effect it expects to have on the ecosystem, and this one did neither. One of the rationales goes further and records the disagreement inside the committee: the members who found the action constitutional acknowledged those same gaps but did not think a missing plan was enough to justify a veto, since a body of a few people holding one should use it only for clear violations.

## What may flow out

With the inflow settled, the question became the outflow, and the constitution had already answered part of it: no budget request may be made until a net change limit has been agreed, a ceiling on how much the treasury may pay out over a defined period. Without one, both proposals note, a budget request is unconstitutional. Two arrived one epoch apart with different answers.

[Set 2025 Net Change Limit of 300M ADA, 2026 Net Change Limit of 250M ADA](/ga/7f320409d9998712ff3a3cdf0c9439e1543f236a3d746766f78f1fdbe1e06bf800/), filed in epoch 546 by proposers who state they speak for no organisation, asks for ₳300M this year and ₳250M the next, pegged to what the treasury is forecast to take in: ₳300M in 2025 and ₳265M in 2026, the second rounded down to a figure its authors call easier to remember. Its own text invites anyone who wants more to vote for it anyway and raise the ceiling afterwards, on the grounds that no limit at all blocks every budget request, and adds that whichever limit was agreed last stays in force until a new one replaces it.

[2025 Net Change Limit](/ga/9b62b3c632f329016a968ac25211825bb4f84b12461121c7da3aa11df92370f900/) followed in epoch 547 and asks for ₳350M for one period only, beginning at the start of epoch 532 and running 72 epochs to the end of epoch 604. Its figure comes from the other direction, the actual income of the previous year rather than a forecast of this one: modelling in October 2024 had projected treasury growth of about ₳351M across 2024, the inflow from epoch 459 to epoch 531 then came to ₳336.0M, and the document keeps ₳350M because the two are within 5% of each other. The period and the figure were agreed with the budget committee, it says, and the next year should get its own limit rather than being settled now.

The two documents also part ways on what counts as agreement. Both quote the guardrail in the constitution under which a limit is agreed by more than 50% of the active voting stake. The single year proposal takes that as its bar. The two year proposal holds itself to more than 67% instead, to match the bar a treasury withdrawal has to clear. Which bar applies is not the ledger's decision, because an information action has no bar in the protocol at all.

So the choice was between a lower ceiling with the following year already pinned down, and a higher one for a single period with the next left open. Both are information actions, which decide nothing by themselves: the amount only matters because every later withdrawal is measured against whichever limit the DReps endorse. The two year proposal had a deadline at the start of epoch 553 and the single year one at the start of epoch 554, and the [review of epochs 548 to 553](/governance-review/epochs-548-553/) has the first of the two results.

## Participation at a high, concentration at a low

The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 391 at epoch 545 to 400 at epoch 547, the most up to then, and the power delegated to them passed ₳4B, from ₳3.89B to ₳4.10B across 807 DReps and the highest reading up to then. The ten largest held 41.43% of it at epoch 546, the smallest share up to then, while the Gini figure for the whole spread read 0.9203 at epoch 547, the widest up to then, the same pair of readings the [review of epochs 542 to 544](/governance-review/epochs-542-544/) explains. The window recorded 247 ballots, superseded votes included, 216 from DReps, 25 from pools and 6 from committee members, with final ballots from 155 DReps. The treasury reading rose ₳8.6M to ₳1.71B, still with nothing paid out of it.
