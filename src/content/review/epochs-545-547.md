## What flows in stays the same

Each epoch Cardano mints rewards out of its reserves, adds the transaction fees, and takes a fifth off the top for the treasury before the rest is paid to stakers. [Decrease Treasury Tax from 20% to 10%](/ga/941502b0aa104c850d197923259444d2b57cab7af18b63143775465aaacc84f500/) asked for that fifth to become a tenth, arguing that the rate had been set at launch and never revisited. It expired at the start of epoch 546 with 23.22% of DRep power behind it, well short of the 67% a change to an economic parameter needs, on 140 yes ballots, 223 no and 11 abstain, ₳0.91B of power for it against ₳2.02B. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no.

## What the treasury is for

The DReps who refused the cut wrote about what the treasury still has to pay for. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/), the largest, listed "a lot of fundamental research and development" still ahead, from scalability and partner chains to alternative nodes, governance tools and research into node memory. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) put it as a matter of who pays: taxing the rewards of large entities and putting them to work for Cardano "is more important than a small percentage gain for each individual" holder. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) wrote that the scalability era "is not complete" and that an extra 6 ada per 100,000 staked "makes no sense" if the price falls. [BTBF_XSP](/dreps/drep1y2vyrqh0fa4qh4tmy7322va6ryk78uh6szppezaft0watyqp5hdwx/) wanted the parameters simulated again first, since they "are intricately interconnected". Pi Lanningham voted no because raising the rate later "will be harder" than lowering it now. Hephaestus Stake Pool questioned the arithmetic: staking returns are nearer 2.5% to 3% than the 4% the proposal assumed, and the Cardano Foundation's report on economic parameters puts the treasury's effective growth rate over the past four years nearer 25% to 35% than the 20% the parameter alone suggests.

The yes side argued that the treasury is big enough. [CryptoCrow](/dreps/drep1ytfwpmt2fvdnyvlswyjyggpf8alwkpm7ha6cua9nsak525ssmvm57/) wrote that "we have a substantial treasury already" and that higher rewards would carry pools through a bear market. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) put the gain at approximately 12.5% more in staking rewards and called the 20% rate arbitrary and never reassessed. [Martin Lang](/dreps/drep1ytwmwvtd0a8lr45ssner2tjxzv5y8q03w3606yeald9mdmgmwecja/) wrote that the high rate had done its job of building the treasury, that a lower one would "put pressure on the expenses that are planned for the next year", and that it can always be raised again.

## What the committee said

The committee was against it too, five members to two, a share of 28.57%. What their published reasons say is worth reading carefully, because they are not about whether a lower tax is a good idea. The committee rules only on whether an action is constitutional, and on the number itself the answer was yes: the constitution permits a treasury share anywhere between 10% and 30%, so a tenth is inside the range. The objection was to what the proposal did not contain. A parameter change has to come with a plan for reverting it, and it has to address the effect it expects to have on the ecosystem. The councils that voted no found the first missing, and the Eastern Cardano Council found the second insufficient as well. Input Output found it unconstitutional for "one constitutional requirement: a reversion plan", while calling the proposal a contribution "made in good faith". The Intersect Constitutional Council reached the same finding on a split of four to three, adding a second objection: a justification that assumed staking returns of 4% rising to 4.5% when returns on saturated pools were nearer 2.8%, which its members thought could mislead voters, and it wrote that a corrected resubmission would most likely be found constitutional. The Eastern Cardano Council's rationale also records a split inside that council: two of its members thought the shortcomings insufficient to justify an unconstitutional verdict and four disagreed, the minority arguing that a body of a few people holding a veto should use it only for clear violations. The Cardano Atlantic Council, one of the two yes votes, published its checklist: legible format, minimum rationale, matching content, technical review, an account of expected impact, and a value inside the guardrails.

## What may flow out

With the inflow settled, the question became the outflow, and the constitution had already answered part of it: no budget request may be made until a net change limit has been agreed, a ceiling on how much the treasury may pay out over a defined period. Without one, both proposals note, a budget request is unconstitutional. Two arrived one epoch apart with different answers.

[Set 2025 Net Change Limit of 300M ADA, 2026 Net Change Limit of 250M ADA](/ga/7f320409d9998712ff3a3cdf0c9439e1543f236a3d746766f78f1fdbe1e06bf800/), filed in epoch 546 by proposers who state they speak for no organisation, asks for ₳300M this year and ₳250M the next, pegged to what the treasury is forecast to take in: ₳300M in 2025 and ₳265M in 2026, the second rounded down to a figure its authors call easier to remember. Its own text invites anyone who wants more to vote for it anyway and raise the ceiling afterwards, on the grounds that no limit at all blocks every budget request, and adds that whichever limit was agreed last stays in force until a new one replaces it.

[2025 Net Change Limit](/ga/9b62b3c632f329016a968ac25211825bb4f84b12461121c7da3aa11df92370f900/) followed in epoch 547 and asks for ₳350M for one period only, from the start of epoch 532 to the end of epoch 604, a range its text calls 72 epochs although it covers 73. Its figure comes from the other direction, the actual income of the previous year rather than a forecast of this one: modelling in October 2024 had projected treasury growth of about ₳351M across 2024, the inflow from epoch 459 to epoch 531 then came to ₳336.0M, and the document keeps ₳350M because the two are within 5% of each other. The period and the figure were agreed with the budget committee, it says, and the next year should get its own limit rather than being settled now.

The two documents also part ways on what counts as agreement. Both quote the guardrail in the constitution under which a limit is agreed by more than 50% of the active voting stake. The single year proposal takes that as its bar. The two year proposal holds itself to more than 67% instead, to match the bar a treasury withdrawal has to clear. Which bar applies is not the ledger's decision, because an information action has no bar in the protocol at all.

The first ballots were cast before the window closed, and no share is given for either. CardanoYoda voted yes on the two year limit in epoch 546 while preferring lower figures of its own, 280,000,000 ada and then 230,000,000, on the principle that the limit "does not exceed the expected revenues of the Treasury". Dumpling voted no in epoch 547 because 300,000,000 ada is a very high figure, three community funding rounds of 30,000,000 each coming to 90,000,000, and because nothing yet measures how well treasury money is spent. Martin Lang voted no on the two year limit and yes on the single year one in epoch 547, for a reason that had nothing to do with the sums: the first never defines what a net change limit is, whether the sum of withdrawals or the change in the balance including inflows, and when its year begins, while the second defines both. So the choice was between a lower ceiling with the following year already pinned down, and a higher one for a single period with the next left open. Both are information actions, which decide nothing by themselves: the amount only matters because every later withdrawal is measured against whichever limit the DReps endorse. The two year proposal had a deadline at the start of epoch 553 and the single year one at the start of epoch 554, and the [review of epochs 548 to 553](/governance-review/epochs-548-553/) has the first of the two results.

## Participation at a high, concentration at a low

The count of DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 391 at epoch 545 to 400 at epoch 547, the most up to then, and the power delegated to them passed ₳4B, from ₳3.89B to ₳4.10B across 807 DReps and the highest reading up to then. The ten largest held 41.43% of it at epoch 546, the smallest share up to then, while the Gini figure for the whole spread read 0.9203 at epoch 547, the widest up to then, the same pair of readings the [review of epochs 542 to 544](/governance-review/epochs-542-544/) explains. The window recorded 247 ballots, superseded votes included, 216 from DReps, 25 from pools and 6 from committee members, with final ballots from 155 DReps. The treasury reading rose ₳8.6M to ₳1.71B, still with nothing paid out of it.
