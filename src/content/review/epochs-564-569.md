## A budget is not a payment

[Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem Budget - 275M ada Administered by Intersect](/ga/e14de8d9dc4f4ddf3fe9250a8a926e20f10e99b86bd0610b77d7a054981591ee00/) closed at the start of epoch 564 with 65.4% of DRep power behind it, on 193 yes ballots, 63 no and 13 abstain, ₳3.51B of power for it against ₳0.71B, with six committee members voting yes and one abstaining. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. Not one ada moved as a result.

That is the design, not a delay. A budget names what may be funded and by whom it will be administered, and being paid is a second, separate act: a treasury withdrawal action for a specific amount to a specific address, which DReps and the committee vote on in its own right. Nothing can be withdrawn that a budget did not cover, and nothing a budget covers is withdrawn until someone asks. The DReps who voted yes said as much themselves. The [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) called the action "just pro forma", since each withdrawal would still need 67%, "restrictive enough". AdaStat wrote that the step "mostly adds delays and confusion" and voted yes to let projects move forward. [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) called it "a constitutionally required checkpoint" and a prerequisite for any withdrawal, and [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) supported the structure it puts around 39 proposals, with multisig wallets and milestone tracking under Intersect as administrator.

The largest yes came with a condition. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) wrote that withdrawals "must be performed in reasonably granular chunks", that any unreasonably aggregated withdrawal "will be immediately voted" against, and that large proposals with several parts should be broken down and put to the DReps item by item before the money is drawn.

## The no votes were about the bundle

The recurring objection in the published no rationales was the bundle: not the projects, but deciding 39 of them in one vote. SASA_nagamaru_ながまる wrote that the spending ceiling should come first and proposals then arrive "in smaller, categorized groups", because "it's not just about what we fund" but how. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) had supported 35 proposals in the earlier off chain signalling, but opposed 14 of the 39 in this bundle, which made the package "unacceptable". Dave objected that the signalling vote was not held on chain and that bundling 39 proposals "forces DReps into a binary, all-or-nothing choice". The Tempo DRep pointed out that many of the bundled proposals had not reached the 50% mark on their own in the signalling vote, and Cerkoryn that ₳275M "feels large for a first cycle" under a process still maturing. hix_coffeepool☕️ voted no on the amount rather than the form, arguing that large withdrawals in a short time could harm the value of ada.

The one committee abstention made a point of its own. The Cardano Japan Council wrote that the budget describes oversight for funds disbursed to outside organisations, but that Intersect is "both the recipient and the overseer of a significant portion" of it, which limits the oversight of the funds it keeps.

The bundle is also not the only budget in force. A ₳1.5M budget for a second Cardano node had closed on its own one window earlier, in the [review of epochs 561 to 563](/governance-review/epochs-561-563/), and the bundle's text excludes every proposal that went on chain alone. So the first withdrawal request ever filed arrived under the smaller budget, not the large one, and the 39 items in the bundle were still waiting for a request of their own at the close of epoch 569.

## The first request

[Amaru Treasury Withdrawal 2025](/ga/60ed6ab43c840ff888a8af30a1ed27b41e9f4a91a89822b2b63d1bfc52aeec4500/) was filed in epoch 566 for ₳1.5M, the first treasury withdrawal request ever filed on Cardano. Its text names the budget it follows, the node budget of the previous window rather than the bundle, and it has to fit under the ₳350M ceiling for the period ending at epoch 604 like every other withdrawal. What it buys is set out scope by scope, each with an owner and its own address: ₳300,000 for the ledger and the overall architecture, ₳300,000 for consensus and simulation testing, ₳500,000 for ad hoc work and middleware integrations, ₳100,000 for project management and marketing, and ₳300,000 held back as a contingency reserve, a quarter of the sum of the rest. The people running those scopes are paid from elsewhere, so the request covers work that nothing else funds. Where the money would sit is spelled out too. The proposers describe their own treasury contract as still under audit and contributor payroll as running out within weeks, so they chose a simplified setup on an already audited contract built for Intersect, to be migrated once their own is ready, with a rule that unspent ada goes back to the treasury after an agreed date.

The first ballots came fast and mostly one way. In its first epoch it drew 53 DRep yes ballots, 1 no and 2 abstain, and by epoch 569 all seven committee members had voted yes on it. YUTA voted yes in epoch 566 because the request corresponds directly to the roadmap the DReps had approved and the cost breakdown "appears efficient". EMURGO followed in epoch 567, calling it "a groundbreaking milestone" for funding a second node through an auditable process, and Yoroi in epoch 568, because a second node reduces reliance on a single implementation of the software. The one sizeable objection in the window came from TriangleForces, who found a full time equivalent valued at 200,000 dollars a year "disproportionate when benchmarked against industry standards". The [review of epochs 570 to 572](/governance-review/epochs-570-572/) has the decision and the payment.

## Two epochs with nothing to vote on

Epochs 564 and 565 record no DRep ballot at all, which reads worse than it is: the budget had closed and the first request had not yet been filed, so there was nothing open for a DRep to vote on. Voting resumed in epoch 566 with 56 ballots. Over the whole window DReps cast 316 ballots, pools 10 and committee members 8, 334 in all with superseded votes included, and 194 distinct DReps cast a final ballot.

## Delegation drops, and the treasury does not

The power delegated to DReps reached ₳5.47B at epoch 564, the highest reading up to then, and read ₳5.19B one epoch later, ₳279.5M less. Nothing left the treasury and no ada was destroyed: delegated power is stake that someone has pointed at a DRep, so a fall in it is less stake associated with DReps, and the snapshot does not show which delegations or balances changed.

Where it went is not something the readings answer, and the arithmetic is worth doing carefully. Across epochs 563 to 569 the total fell ₳104.9M, while over exactly the same stretch the largest single decline the record lists for one DRep is ₳311.1M, from ₳714.4M to ₳403.3M, and that DRep is YUTA, the largest yes voter on the budget. ## Two governance tools ask to be paid

Two more budget signals arrived, both for the software people use to take part in governance rather than for the protocol itself. [Cardano GovTool Budget - 12 months full active maintenance and development](/ga/9d213a57684d7ddf6f3350c80d042639ecbed5ccccc4a05bf54959a086593e7b00/), filed in epoch 567, is for the open source applications used to register as a DRep, delegate, submit proposals and vote: its existing funding was running out, and the consortium of builders behind it asked ₳1.15M for a year, roughly half an earlier version of the same request. The first ballots already split on the price. YUTA voted yes in epoch 568 because barriers to taking part in governance "are still very big" and most wallets give governance functions "low priority", so the tool's services could speed things up. CardanoYoda voted yes in the same epoch after leaning the other way, writing that 1,150,000 ada, about 920,000 dollars for 5.75 full time equivalents, "seemed quite steep" and that 1,000,000 dollars a year "should be enough to support several tools, not just one". Medusa DRep voted no in epoch 569 as a developer who runs a cluster of nodes, an indexer and a frontend for "around $100 per month", against the roughly 27,500 dollars a month the proposal asks for infrastructure and maintenance. The two figures cover different scopes: the proposal's foundational tier, 330,000 dollars a year, pays for a maintenance team of two full time equivalents as well as hosting.

[Tempo for Cardono Governance - Maintenance & Development Budget for 2025](/ga/495bd3cd44ea6bc0b4c2dbb1e4cdc7e214874c2c07490a4e2476a627ef91128000/) followed in epoch 569 for a separate, independent voting application built for phones, which runs its own node rather than depending on somebody else's, and asks ₳380,000 for six months. The [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/) has the first result and the [review of epochs 576 to 578](/governance-review/epochs-576-578/) the second.

The treasury reading rose ₳21.1M to ₳1.80B by epoch 569, the highest up to then, and none of the ₳350M allowed for the period ending at epoch 604 had been drawn.
