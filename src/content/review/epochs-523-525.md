## One update, submitted twice

Both parameter changes decided at the start of epoch 525 asked for the same thing. The coming hard fork would add 12 new operations to Plutus, the language Cardano smart contracts are written in, most of them for working on individual bits and one a hash function Bitcoin relies on. Every operation needs a price in the cost model before a script may call it, and neither proposal touched the price of anything that already existed. Without the update the new operations would have shipped unusable. The document also records where the change came from: a parameter committee at Intersect had recommended it and its technical steering committee had endorsed it before it was filed, and the operations themselves were to become available only with the hard fork to protocol version 10.

[A parameter change without a title in the record](/ga/51f495aa23f4b3b3aa90afde4a0e67823bb7ac4ac65f5ffbb138373b863f2f7400/) was the first attempt, submitted in epoch 518. Its proposers have since written that shortly after submitting it they found it would not have the effect they wanted on chain, and that they broke its metadata address deliberately, by renaming the directory it pointed at, which is why the record holds no title and no text for it at all. The document still sits at the renamed address, with the same title and abstract as its replacement. The committee voted it down: Cardano Atlantic Council and Eastern Cardano Council in epoch 520, Cardano Foundation and Emurgo in epoch 521, Intersect Constitutional Council in epoch 524. Two members cast no ballot, nobody voted yes, and with seven active members and no abstainer the share stood at 0% against a two thirds bar when it expired at the start of epoch 525.

The published no votes say what a broken action looks like from the committee's side. The Eastern Cardano Council found "the absence of sufficient rationale", because the link the action carried no longer resolved. The Cardano Atlantic Council found that the action "does not propose any changes to the network parameters" at all, in conflict with its own stated goal. The Intersect Constitutional Council recorded its understanding of what had gone wrong: the changes had been submitted from an older node version, which discarded the entries a newer one would have carried, so the action was empty as well as unexplained.

[Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/), filed one epoch later, was ratified at that same boundary. Five members had voted yes by epoch 524, which for seven active members with nobody abstaining was already past the bar, and two more yes ballots are dated to epoch 525 itself. The yes votes read as a checklist against the guardrails. Emurgo cited three of them: cost model values must be set by benchmarking on a reference architecture, the model must be updated when new primitives arrive, and no value may be negative. The Cardano Atlantic Council added that the changes matched what had been circulated off chain and had been deployed safely to the test networks. Input Output called enabling the new Plutus features "a valid purpose" for a technical parameter change, and the Intersect Constitutional Council noted the unanimous endorsement of the technical steering committee. It took effect one epoch after ratification, in the [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/).

Neither change drew a DRep or a pool ballot, and neither could have. In this phase of governance a parameter change rested on the committee alone, which is why the table under this article shows no DRep share for either rather than a share of nought.

## A hard fork gets a name

The one proposal open to DReps in these three epochs was not about parameters at all. [Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/), filed by a hard fork working group at Intersect, asks for the coming upgrade to carry the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang, and places it in a tradition of naming hard forks in memory of contributors to Cardano. An information action of this sort registers a signal and is never ratified, so a yes is an endorsement of the name and nothing else, and no share is given here.

The DReps who voted in these epochs wrote about the man. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/), the largest, voted yes in epoch 524 because the name had "widespread support" and keeping the old one "would cause more confusion", adding a note for the future that a name should not be changed just before a hard fork. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) wrote in epoch 525 that he "embodied the values and ideals of the community". Cerkoryn called him "one of the greatest visionaries" it had known, and Duc Tiger placed the name in Cardano's tradition of naming upgrades after people who built it. Its voting ran to the start of epoch 529, and the [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/) covers its last three epochs.

## The busiest epoch yet

DReps cast 71 ballots in epoch 523, the most in a single epoch up to then and almost as many as the 73 recorded across the previous six epochs, then 20 and 35, with pools adding 36, 4 and 4. None of those ballots was on either parameter change, so the rename signal absorbed them. At epoch 525, 569 DReps held ₳1.60B of delegated power, ₳158.8M more than at epoch 523 and the highest reading up to then, the count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 163 to 181, and the share of the ten largest DReps fell from 46.76% to 45.68%. The treasury gained ₳9.0M to reach ₳1.61B, with no withdrawal paid out of it.
