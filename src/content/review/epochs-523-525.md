## Intersect's first filing expires, the second is ratified

Intersect's first filing expired at the start of epoch 525, and its second filing of the same update to the Plutus V3 cost model was ratified at that boundary. The coming hard fork to protocol version 10 would add 12 new operations to Plutus, the language Cardano smart contracts are written in, most of them for working on individual bits and one a hash function Bitcoin uses. A script may call an operation only once the cost model gives it a price, and the update added prices for the 12 while leaving every existing price unchanged.

[Intersect's first filing](/ga/51f495aa23f4b3b3aa90afde4a0e67823bb7ac4ac65f5ffbb138373b863f2f7400/) was submitted in epoch 518. Intersect's governance actions repository records that the action, if enacted, would not have had the desired effect on chain. Intersect then broke its metadata address on purpose by renaming the directory it pointed at, which is why the data holds no title for it. The document still sits at the renamed address, with the same title and abstract as the second filing. The Intersect Constitutional Council wrote that, as it understood it, the change had been submitted from an older node version that discarded the entries a newer one would have kept, and the Cardano Atlantic Council that the action "does not propose any changes to the network parameters".

Five members found it unconstitutional: the Cardano Atlantic Council and the Eastern Cardano Council in epoch 520, the Cardano Foundation and Emurgo in epoch 521, and the Intersect Constitutional Council in epoch 524. The Eastern Cardano Council and Emurgo each cited "the absence of sufficient rationale", as the address the action carried returned no document when they voted. Input Output and the Cardano Japan Council cast no ballot, and with no member finding it constitutional the action expired at the start of epoch 525.

[Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/), the second filing, was submitted in epoch 519 and ratified at the start of epoch 525. In the bootstrap phase a committee vote alone was sufficient to change protocol parameters, so the table under this article shows no DRep share for either filing. By the end of epoch 524 five members had found it constitutional: the Eastern Cardano Council in epoch 521, Input Output in epoch 522, the Cardano Foundation in epoch 523, and the Cardano Atlantic Council and the Intersect Constitutional Council in epoch 524. With seven active members and no abstainer, five yes ballots were above the committee's 66.7% threshold. The Cardano Japan Council and Emurgo added yes ballots in epoch 525, after that boundary.

Voting yes, the Cardano Atlantic Council wrote that the changes matched what had been circulated off chain and had been deployed safely to the test networks, and Input Output that enabling Plutus V3 features was "a valid purpose" for a change to technical parameters. Emurgo cited three guardrails of the interim constitution: cost model values must be set by benchmarking on a reference architecture, the cost model must be updated when new primitives are introduced, and values should not be negative.

## Intersect's Hard Fork Working Group proposes the name Plomin

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) was the only proposal open to a DRep vote in these three epochs. Intersect filed it in epoch 522 for its Hard Fork Working Group, with Adam Dean and Adam Rusch as authors, to give the coming hard fork, then called Chang 2, the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, as the previous one had been named after Phil Chang. As an information action it closes without ratification, and its voting ran to the start of epoch 529.

## DReps cast 71 ballots in epoch 523

The 71 DRep ballots of epoch 523 were the most in any epoch since epoch 508. Across the three epochs DReps cast 126 ballots, pools 44 and committee members 8. Neither filing holds a DRep or pool ballot, so the DRep and pool ballots all went to the rename signal. At epoch 525, 569 DReps held ₳1.60B of delegated power, ₳158.8M more than at epoch 523 and the highest reading since on-chain governance began. DReps with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 163 to 181, and the share of the ten largest DReps fell from 46.8% to 45.7%. The treasury reading rose ₳9.0M to ₳1.61B, with no withdrawal paid from it.
