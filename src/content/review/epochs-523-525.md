## One update, submitted twice

Both parameter changes decided at the start of epoch 525 asked for the same thing. The coming hard fork would add 12 new operations to Plutus, the language Cardano smart contracts are written in, most of them for working on individual bits and one a hash function Bitcoin relies on. Every operation needs a price in the cost model before a script may call it, and neither proposal touched the price of anything that already existed. Without the update the new operations would have shipped unusable.

[A parameter change without a title in the record](/ga/51f495aa23f4b3b3aa90afde4a0e67823bb7ac4ac65f5ffbb138373b863f2f7400/) was the first attempt, submitted in epoch 518. Its proposers have since written that shortly after submitting it they found it would not have the effect they wanted on chain, and that they broke its metadata address deliberately, by renaming the directory it pointed at, which is why the record holds no title and no text for it at all. The committee voted it down: Cardano Atlantic Council and Eastern Cardano Council in epoch 520, Cardano Foundation and Emurgo in epoch 521, Intersect Constitutional Council in epoch 524. Two members cast no ballot, nobody voted yes, and with seven active members and no abstainer the share stood at 0% against a two thirds bar when it expired at the start of epoch 525.

[Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/), filed one epoch later, was ratified at that same boundary. Five members had voted yes by epoch 524, which for seven active members with nobody abstaining was already past the bar, and two more yes ballots are dated to epoch 525 itself. It took effect one epoch after ratification, in the [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/).

Neither change drew a DRep or a pool ballot, and neither could have. In this phase of governance a parameter change rested on the committee alone, which is why the table under this article shows no DRep share for either rather than a share of nought.

## A hard fork gets a name

The one proposal open to DReps in these three epochs was not about parameters at all. [Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) asks for the coming upgrade to carry the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang. An information action of this sort registers a signal and is never ratified, so a yes is an endorsement of the name and nothing else. Its voting ran to the start of epoch 529, and the [review of epochs 526 to 528](/governance-review/epochs-526-528/) covers its last three epochs.

## The busiest epoch yet

DReps cast 71 ballots in epoch 523, the most in a single epoch up to then and more than the whole of the previous window, then 20 and 35, with pools adding 36, 4 and 4. None of those ballots was on either parameter change, so the rename signal absorbed them. At epoch 525, 569 DReps held ₳1.60B of delegated power, ₳158.8M more than at epoch 523 and the highest reading up to then, the count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 163 to 181, and the share of the ten largest DReps fell from 46.76% to 45.68%. The treasury gained ₳9.0M to reach ₳1.61B, with no withdrawal paid out of it.
