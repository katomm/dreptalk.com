## The rules change at one boundary

At the start of epoch 537 [Hard Fork to Protocol Version 10 ("Plomin" Hard Fork)](/ga/0b19476e40bbbb5e1e8ce153523762e2b6859e7ecacbaf06eae0ee6a447e79b900/) took effect, and the list of things Cardano governance could decide stopped being a short one. Until that boundary a proposal could adjust a protocol parameter, start a hard fork, or record an opinion, and nothing else: no payment out of the treasury, no change to the constitutional committee, no constitution of its own. From the boundary on, all of those were possible, and none of them could be settled without the DReps, who until then had voted only on information actions that bound nobody.

The proof arrived immediately, and it had a deadline of its own. [Cardano Constitution to Replace the Interim Constitution](/ga/8c653ee5c9800e6d31e79b5a7f7d4400c81d44717ad4db633dc18d4c07e4a4fd00/) was submitted in epoch 537 itself, the first proposal of a kind that had been impossible one epoch earlier. Its document explains the timing: the interim constitution had required its own replacement to be put on chain no later than 31 January 2025, and this proposal went on chain on 30 January, one day inside that limit and in the first epoch a constitution could be proposed at all. The text it carried had been approved by elected delegates at a convention in December 2024, so what the hard fork unlocked was not the drafting but the vote. DReps cast 142 yes ballots on it in that epoch against 3 no and 1 abstain, and the largest of them said why on the first day. [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) wrote that the text "clarifies rules, prevents arbitrary interpretation, and strengthens financial protections" and that rejecting it would mean a costly reconstitution. [SIPO](/dreps/drep1yffld2866p00cyg3ejjdewtvazgah7jjgk0s9m7m5ytmmdq33v3zh/) called it "the foundation for true decentralization". Its deadline was the start of epoch 544, though it could be ratified at an earlier epoch boundary once it met its thresholds and the other requirements, and the [review of epochs 538 to 541](/governance-review/epochs-538-541/) has the decision and the reasons on both sides.

## How the hard fork itself was decided

The upgrade that gave DReps their vote was one they had no vote on. It needed the committee and the stake pools, and its own document added two conditions to be met before ratification: more than 90 days since the Chang upgrade, and at least 85% of pools by stake running a node able to support the new version, which the committee and the pools were to verify from readiness reports. It got both bodies: 65.99% of counted pool stake against a 51% bar, with abstaining stake set aside and stake that cast no ballot counted against, plus 4 pool no ballots and 35 abstentions. All seven committee members voted yes, two in epoch 534 and the other five in epoch 535. Ratification followed at the start of epoch 536 and the upgrade itself one epoch later, the interval every ratified action waits before it takes effect.

The committee members who published reasons treated the vote as a checklist. Input Output wrote that more than 90 days had passed since the Chang hard fork, that the technical review in that time had included monitoring of live networks, independent testing, a regression suite and benchmark reporting, and that at least 85% of pools by active stake were on a capable node. The Intersect Constitutional Council added the interim requirement that DReps have had at least 18 epochs to register and campaign, and the Eastern Cardano Council the guardrail that a hard fork not endanger the security, functionality or performance of the chain. Among the pools, one published its own readiness check before voting yes: 61.26% of exchange liquidity reported ready with another 24.84% in progress, 86% of that epoch's blocks validated by nodes on the newest version, most wallets and applications ready, and "the only reservation remaining" the distribution of DRep delegation. Another pool voted yes with the same reservation stated more sharply, only 7.5% of ada delegated to DReps against 58% delegated to pools. The [review of epochs 529 to 534](/governance-review/epochs-529-534/) covers the six epochs of pool voting that led here.

## A dormant electorate wakes up in one epoch

The eight epochs before this boundary hold no DRep ballot at all, and at epoch 536, 575 of the 673 DReps holding delegated power had none in the previous twelve epochs. That was not indifference: for eight epochs there had been nothing open they were entitled to vote on. The moment there was, the count of DReps with a recent ballot went from 99 at epoch 536 to 190 at epoch 537, and 137 distinct DReps cast a final ballot inside this window.

At the epoch 537 reading, 686 DReps held ₳3.00B of delegated power, ₳161.4M more than at epoch 535 and the highest reading up to then. The window recorded 243 ballots across the roles, superseded votes included: 146 from DReps, 92 from pools and 5 from committee members. The treasury reading rose ₳8.9M to ₳1.66B, still with nothing paid out of it, though for the first time a proposal could now ask.
