## More heads for yes, more power for no

[Should K increased?](/ga/7fd6429add8f2611ad8d48c0cc49101463093aec285faea402e8cfde78ea58d700/), the question whether the cap on how much stake one pool may hold should be raised, closed at the start of epoch 521. More DReps had voted for it than against, 88 to 54, with 22 abstaining. It closed at 15.48% of counted DRep power all the same, because the 54 no ballots carried ₳347.9M of delegated power and the 88 yes ballots ₳153.1M, with ₳119.0M abstaining.

That gap between heads and weight is the first thing a reader of Cardano governance has to get used to, and the share above shows it twice over. A DRep votes with the power delegated to it, so a handful of large DReps outweigh dozens of small ones. And the share is not yes power over all power that voted: abstaining power is set aside entirely, while power that never cast a ballot and power parked on the always no confidence option are counted on the no side. Nothing in the record says why the larger DReps were against. A proposal of this kind was never going to change the parameter in any case: it registers an opinion, and its own text says it was filed to get people voting before the next hard fork rather than to move the cap.

## The note of thanks closes at 41.42%

[Cardanoの生きがい - Ikigai -](/ga/59fd353253eb177e2104e8f23ea4c63e3d32ef95c7865d03e90d3884424dc1db00/), the proposal that thanks the organisations which brought Cardano into the world and asks for no change at all, closed at the start of epoch 517 on 104 yes ballots, 3 no and 16 abstain, for 41.42% of counted DRep power. Pools recorded 44 yes ballots to 1 no, a yes stake share the record puts at 3.75%. The committee ended at 75%: three members voted yes, three abstained and the seventh cast no ballot, and because abstainers drop out of the sum while a member who never votes does not, that last silence cost more than the three abstentions.

## Three filings that would matter more

The three proposals submitted here all outlived the window, and unlike the two signals just closed, two of them could change something. A parameter change the record stores without a title arrived in epoch 518 and had collected four committee no votes by epoch 521. It asked for the same Plutus cost model update as [Plutus V3 Cost Model Parameter Changes Prior to Chang#2](/ga/b2a591ac219ce6dcca5847e0248015209c7cb0436aa6bd6863d0c1f152a60bc500/), submitted one epoch later, which had two committee yes votes by epoch 522. That update is a price list. The coming hard fork would add 12 new operations to Plutus, the language Cardano smart contracts are written in, bitwise operations and a hash function that Bitcoin uses, and a contract may only call an operation the cost model has a price for, so the entries had to be in place before the hard fork raised the protocol version to 10. The price of everything already in use was left as it was, and the document records that a parameter committee at Intersect had recommended the change and its technical steering committee had endorsed it before it was filed. The proposers of the first attempt have since written that they found a flaw shortly after submitting it and broke its metadata address on purpose, which is why the record has no title for it and the site cannot show what it asked for. Neither drew a DRep or pool ballot, because in this phase a parameter change was the committee's decision alone.

[Rename the Chang 2 Hard Fork to the Plomin Hard Fork](/ga/fff0df644d328a5367212f45bab59060bde3c4091dc96c723062896fd619731400/) followed in epoch 522 with three DRep yes ballots and 25 pool yes ballots in that epoch. It was filed by a hard fork working group at Intersect and asks for the coming upgrade to carry the name of Matthew Plomin, a community contributor who died in November 2024, in the way the previous one was named after Phil Chang. It was the second proposal in this record to put a name to that hard fork: the first, which asked for it to be named after a token, had closed at the start of epoch 514 with little DRep power behind it, as the [review of epochs 514 to 516](/governance-review/epochs-514-516/) reports. The [review of epochs 523 to 525](/governance-review/epochs-523-525/) has the two parameter decisions.

## Delegated power nearly doubles

The power delegated to DReps grew ₳576.8M between epochs 517 and 522, from ₳0.65B to ₳1.23B, the highest reading up to then, spread across 518 DReps. Voting did not keep pace: 371 of those 518 had cast no ballot in the previous twelve epochs, and recorded DRep ballots per epoch ran 19, 15, 16, 20, 0 and 3, with epoch 521 the first in the series to record none at all. Of the 154 ballots cast across the window, superseded votes included, 73 came from DReps, 70 from pools and 11 from committee members, with final ballots from 70 distinct DReps and 65 pools. The treasury stood at ₳1.60B at the epoch 522 reading, ₳22.8M above its epoch 517 reading, and no withdrawal had been paid out of it.
