## More power for than against, and no money

Two requests from the first spending round expired at the start of epoch 577, and both had more delegated power voting for them than against.

[the quadratic funding request](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e1b/) closed at 57.32% of DRep power, on 124 yes ballots to 81 no and 15 abstain. [the tokenised property request](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e1e/) closed at 52.72%, on 93 yes to 113 no and 20 abstain. The shares in this edition are the yes power as a share of all counted power, with abstaining power left out and power that cast no ballot counted as no. A treasury withdrawal needs 67% of that, and neither got near it.

The second case is the sharper one. Fewer DReps voted for it than against, and it still had a majority of weight, because the yes ballots came from the larger DReps. The first shows the other pattern, a comfortable lead among voters that still comes to 57.32% of counted power, because power that cast no ballot counts as no. Neither failed on constitutionality: all seven committee members found both of them constitutional, which is the committee checking proposals against the rules and saying nothing about whether a project is worth ₳3.0M.

## A live trial was already running

The funding request would have paid ₳1.5M for a grant mechanism to run alongside the existing community programme, using quadratic funding and a reputation score so that many small supporters count for more than a few large ones, and its text makes a point of taking no operational cut. Yoroi, EMURGO, the Eternl DRep Committee and the Cardano Foundation voted yes, and YUTA voted no with its standard list of criteria and no reason specific to the request. [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/) called it "a larger-scale testbed for governance innovation" and [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) "a timely contribution" to the infrastructure around Catalyst, with reviewer pairing and impact reporting that could help with quality control and fake identities.

The no votes were about timing and about principle. Dave wrote that the fourteenth Catalyst funding round was already trying quadratic voting at its tallying stage as a proof of concept, and that it is "more prudent to first analyze the valuable data from this live experiment" than to fund a separate initiative. Hephaestus Stake Pool made the same comparison from the launch guide of that round, which describes generalised quadratic voting as a proof of concept alongside the existing vote. HOSKY voted no on principle: weighting donations by their square root and adding reputation scores shifts influence away from proportional ada ownership, and one ada should equal one vote. [CardanoYoda (MANDA Pool)](/dreps/drep1y2m0g4r66pyaw3p7u454wc0p4f0ygm8ueaev0mgd3tvwm7sskqwqp/) scored it 4 out of 12 on its own scale, with 0 for value for money. The Tempo DRep wrote that the cost was too high for a quadratic voting implementation and that Catalyst's own operating funding should cover it.

## A company that should fund itself

The property request would have paid ₳3.0M for a protocol that sells fractions of the equity in a house, prototyped on a private chain elsewhere and proposed for Cardano. The rationales name the company, Haus, which the proposal's title does not. Yoroi voted yes because it has "a working product, a sizable waitlist" and regulatory groundwork under way, EMURGO because tokenising home equity "unlocks liquidity for homeowners" and the project promises to donate back to the treasury, and [CryptoCrow](/dreps/drep1ytfwpmt2fvdnyvlswyjyggpf8alwkpm7ha6cua9nsak525ssmvm57/) because real world assets will "pick up the pace a lot between now and 2030".

The opponents asked instead whether the treasury should finance the company's expansion at all. Dave wrote that the treasury is "for public goods, not to fund the expansion of a successful, for-profit company", and that the proposal "socializes their business risk while privatizing the profits". HOSKY quoted the team saying it was "giving Cardano a chance" despite larger offers elsewhere, and read that as opportunism rather than commitment. Kyle Solomon abstained after meeting the team at a conference, writing that the company should be a venture capital play and not a treasury one. Hephaestus Stake Pool found some of the supporting documentation behind a request for an email address, the only proposal of the round to do so. The Tempo DRep wrote that Cardano has no decentralised oracle and is not ready for real world assets, and that ₳3M "is nothing in the real estate market". CardanoYoda gave it the same 4 out of 12 as the funding request, with 0 for infrastructure.

## The narrowest pass

[the listing and market making request](/ga/8ad3d454f3496a35cb0d07b0fd32f687f66338b7d60e787fc0a22939e5d8833e1c/), ₳3,126,000 to pay for exchange listings and market making for a set of Cardano tokens, closed at 67.37% of DRep power, on 115 yes ballots to 96 no, and was paid at the start of epoch 578. No other request of the round came as close to its bar. Six committee members found it constitutional and one did not: the Eastern Cardano Council cited the requirement that a proposal provide sufficient rationale in a standardised format.

The no ballots came from DReps who would have benefited and from DReps who saw a precedent. HOSKY voted no although the proposal lists its own token as eligible, writing that listing fees are "primarily a private benefit to the projects being listed" and should be paid by those projects, their investors or their communities. Dave objected to the cost and to a dependency on a single project, DexHunter, for infrastructure. Hephaestus Stake Pool wrote that once exchanges see listings paid from a treasury they will expect the same for every Cardano token. [BTBF_XSP](/dreps/drep1y2vyrqh0fa4qh4tmy7322va6ryk78uh6szppezaft0watyqp5hdwx/) called it the only proposal of the round with "limited cost-effectiveness".

## A second governance tool asks for a budget

[Tempo for Cardono Governance - Maintenance & Development Budget for 2025](/ga/495bd3cd44ea6bc0b4c2dbb1e4cdc7e214874c2c07490a4e2476a627ef91128000/) closed at the start of epoch 576 with 40.95% of DRep power behind it, on 119 yes ballots to 75 no and 22 abstain, and the committee split: three members found it constitutional and four did not. Tempo is the second interface DReps use to vote, alongside the one whose own budget passed in epoch 574 in the [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/). The request, as Socious describes it in its yes vote, was 380,000 ada over six months for infrastructure, a better user experience and a lending feature to help proposers cover the deposit.

Yoroi and EMURGO voted no on the paperwork rather than the tool. Yoroi wrote that it "values the spirit behind Tempo" but that the proposal offers "limited visibility into the team behind the project", no accountability framework and no account of how Selfdriven, the named administrator, would govern the funds. EMURGO wrote that a budget action must meet "a higher threshold of clarity and accountability" and that this one had no roadmap, no team details and no metrics for success. CardanoYoda did the arithmetic: 200,000 ada for six months of infrastructure is about 33,333 ada a month, around 25,000 dollars, which "feels excessive", and a comparable proposal "could be executed with a budget up to ten times lower".

The yes votes were about having two tools. The [Cardano Foundation](/dreps/drep1ydpfkyjxzeqvalf6fgvj7lznrk8kcmfnvy9hyl6gr6ez6wgsjaelx/) called its vote pragmatic: the proposal "has flaws in its quality and detail", but the risk of "a tooling monoculture" outweighs them. SASA_nagamaru_ながまる wrote that governance relies on only two active interfaces and that depending on one "would undermine resilience and decentralization". [YUTA](/dreps/drep1y2200we9c904un36tzaearntzzl63snffuul9qsk0te4utqfkke0w/) voted yes on condition that the later withdrawal include open sourcing plans and a roadmap, and the [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) voted yes as a signal of support even if the committee were to find the action unconstitutional. The committee members who did disagreed among themselves about which rule applied: the Cardano Atlantic Council found no process for overseeing the funds, the Cardano Japan Council found that the addresses receiving them were not disclosed and so could not be checked for the automatic abstention the constitution requires, and the Eastern Cardano Council found the action constitutional precisely because it names an administrator.

## The round finishes paying

The payments came in three batches: 27 withdrawals at the start of epoch 576, two more at epoch 577 and five at epoch 578, ₳102.1M across the window. That takes the total drawn against the ceiling to ₳272.0M out of ₳350M, across 38 paid withdrawals since the first one at epoch 571, and leaves ₳78.0M for the rest of the period ending at epoch 604. Of the 39 requests in the round, 37 were paid and the two above were not.

The ada that leaves the treasury is not yet with the vendor. The documents of the requests Intersect submitted on behalf of a vendor all describe the same route: the ada goes into a treasury reserve contract run by Intersect, milestones, acceptance criteria and payment dates are fixed in a contract with each vendor, a third party signs off each milestone, and only then is the money disbursed. The treasury reading fell ₳15.6M over the same three epochs, far less than the ₳102.1M paid, because most of the payments fell in epoch 576 while income kept arriving in all three.

## What the next window decides

Four proposals were open at the close, and two of them would change the rules the round just finished under. [Replace Interim Constitutional Committee](/ga/47a0e7a4f9383b1afc2192b23b41824d65ac978d7741aca61fc1fa16833d111100/) would put an elected committee in place of the one appointed for the trial phase. Its document says the appointed committee's term ends with epoch 580, which is also the action's deadline, and that the seven members chosen in an election held off chain take staggered seats, three for 146 epochs and four for 73, three of them already members of the interim committee. It is the one action of the four on which pools have a vote.

[CARDANO BLOCKCHAIN ECOSYSTEM CONSTITUTION v2.0](/ga/207b226e110e13bb18b119fcd313520e0fcd060b2bc9fb9a5e5bc6e94ab10f3b00/) would revise the constitution ratified eight windows earlier, with a deadline at the start of epoch 581. Its document lists wording, definitions and the removal of unused provisions, and one change its document singles out as substantive: dropping the requirement that a treasury withdrawal be preceded by a budget information action. That is the requirement every one of the 39 requests of this round was filed under, each document stating that it gives effect to the budget agreed in epoch 564. The two requests for the token listing plan covered in the [review of epochs 573 to 575](/governance-review/epochs-573-575/) also ran to the start of epoch 580. The [review of epochs 579 to 581](/governance-review/epochs-579-581/) has all four results.

## Why the pools turn up

The window recorded 921 ballots, 646 from DReps, 260 from stake pools and 15 from committee members. Ballots from pools are the surprise, after three from pools in the whole of the previous window: pools have no vote on a treasury withdrawal, but they do vote on a change to the committee, and 260 of them used it. On the DRep side the count with a ballot in the previous twelve epochs rose from 343 to 378, so the falling ballot totals through the window are a matter of fewer proposals being open rather than fewer DReps taking part. The power delegated to DReps rose ₳19.2M across the window to ₳5.48B, held by 848 DReps at epoch 578, the most up to then.
