## Two ceilings, and one budget between them

Both proposals for the treasury spending limit of epochs 613 to 713 start from the same number. The ₳350M ceiling the DReps agreed in the [window before](/governance-review/epochs-612-614/) takes the treasury's inflows of 2025, ₳306.9M across epochs 532 to 604, and sets the limit at about 115% of them, and its document says what the room above the inflows is for: the ₳50M stablecoin liquidity budget the DReps had approved in 2025 and nobody had yet drawn on. [Net Change Limit of 300 Million ADA for Epochs 613 to 713](/ga/73a4eb2148781c37ef37c90a33a1d3d00511a8eefe9cdfaa1ea593b090f23f9600/) takes the same ₳306.9M and sets the limit at the inflows, no higher, and says it would supersede any limit previously agreed for the period. Read together, the ₳50M between them is the question whether the period holds room for that one budget. The first draw on it, [Cardano Defi Liquidity Budget - Withdrawal 1](/ga/e5c4d7ebaad03ac127ec19a286605a188d6fea1c54e075c619ca6993b7fa9fd100/) for ₳800,000 to set up the legal entity and audit the contract the budget needs, was filed in epoch 617 while the lower ceiling was still being voted on, and names that budget as its basis.

Voting on the lower ceiling closed at the start of epoch 618. The tally was 118 yes votes, 42 no and 13 abstain, more DReps for it than against, and the yes side represented 44.08% of counted DRep power.

Yes ballots carried ₳2.46B, more than the ₳1.71B behind the no ballots. The share is lower than either figure suggests because abstaining power leaves the denominator while delegated power that cast no ballot at all is counted against, and most delegated power did not vote. Among the twelve largest DReps on its ballot list, ranked by delegated power at epoch 620, [Yoroi W₳llet](/dreps/drep1ygr9tuapcanc3kpeyy4dc3vmrz9cfe5q7v9wj3x9j0ap3tswtre9j/), [EMURGO](/dreps/drep1ytvlwvyjmzfyn56n0zz4f6lj94wxhmsl5zky6knnzrf4jygpyahug/) and [Eternl DRep Committee](/dreps/drep1y29h2q6cst2pvkl2sqqvf5ljcy36uv7pmy482xnczddzgqshus24w/) voted no, one abstained and eight voted yes, and at epoch 620 those three held ₳1.28B in delegated power between them.

So does the ₳350M ceiling stand? Yes, and that is the answer the article owes the reader rather than the observation that information actions are not enacted. A ceiling has a bar of its own: the treasury guardrails require more than half of the active voting stake to agree one, and a published account of this vote names that requirement explicitly. At 44.08% the lower ceiling did not reach it, so the ₳350M agreed for epochs 613 to 713 remains the limit in force, and the six committee yes votes on this proposal change nothing about that.

## Nothing paid, one ratification, five new requests

The treasury paid nothing in the six epochs, and its balance rose by ₳19.3M between the readings at epoch 615 and epoch 620, from ₳1.64B to ₳1.66B. Nothing had yet been drawn against the new spending period.

[Amaru Treasury Withdrawal 2026](/ga/2f2fc2539dde550d3c83bea67c11b03861407976bbe8d07c5b0e124aa8a12baa00/) was ratified at the start of epoch 620 with 70.93% of counted DRep power, on 136 yes ballots to 16 no, five committee members in favour and one abstaining. It asks ₳10.1M for a second year of work on Amaru, the independently built block producing node whose first ₳1.5M was the [first payment this record holds](/governance-review/epochs-570-572/). The step from ₳1.5M to ₳10.1M is the difference between funding the scopes that nothing else covered and funding a full year of the project. The document prices that year at fifty cents per ada and counts January and February 2026 into it after the fact, because, it says, the request had been held back until a spending limit with room for it was in place. It promises a relay capable node in the first quarter and a block producing node by the middle of the year, and puts the money under the project's own maintainer committee, paid out through a smart contract that can return unspent contingency to the treasury. It was ratified inside this window and had not been paid at its close.

Five proposals arrived during the window and were still open at its close, among them three withdrawals asking for ₳57.7M together. The largest is ₳50M for [Cardano x Draper Dragon: Orion Fund](/ga/8807e3cee3be5647742c329c1dea242f0c1c29b8ad050e15169e3b0302fcbfd600/), filed in epoch 618 as the first tranche of an investment fund whose document sizes the long term plan at eighty million dollars or more, seventy five million of it from the treasury if later tranches are approved, each by a vote of its own, over six to eight years. The other two came in epoch 617 with deadlines at the start of epoch 624: the ₳800,000 liquidity draw above, and [Dingo: a Production-Grade Block Producer in Go by Blink Labs](/ga/f35285db3c4e085ad331843b3007737952b8a322bb3216311edc37fdf44ad3da00/), ₳6.9M for twelve months of work on a block producing node written in Go, which its document places next to Amaru as a second independent implementation.

Across the six epochs DReps cast 709 ballots, committee members 24 and pools 55, superseded votes included. The 788 ballots include final votes from 180 DReps and 21 pools.
